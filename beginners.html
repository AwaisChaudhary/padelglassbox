Your first padel match.

Quick answer

For your first padel match you need a padel racket (clubs often rent them), padel balls, court shoes with grip, sports clothes and water, plus a booked court and three other players. Know four rules: serve underhand and diagonally, let the ball bounce once on your side, never hit their wall before their ground, and agree the 40-40 rule before you start.

Where can beginners play padel, and what helps?

Booking apps

Many clubs list their courts on booking apps such as Playtomic, where you can also find open matches at your level.

National federations

Your country's padel or tennis federation usually lists clubs and beginner sessions. In the UK, that's LTA Padel.

Join a social night

Many clubs run Americano evenings where you change partner each round. They are a friendly way to meet players at your level. Running one yourself? Use our Americano generator.

Practise between matches

A short practice once a week speeds things up. The drills planner builds a 20 to 60 minute session with a timer.

Know your level

Open matches and clubs often ask for your level. Our padel level calculator gives you a quick estimate.

Know where to stand

Move up and back with your partner, and stay about 1 metre off the back glass. Our positioning guide shows every situation on a court.

Warm up first

Ten minutes of jogging, moving stretches and soft rallies makes the first games easier on your body. Our warm-up timer talks you through it step by step.

Coming from tennis or pickleball?

Your hands will help, but the walls, the underhand serve and the scoring work a little differently. See padel vs tennis vs pickleball for the key changes.

Pick the right shoes

Grip matters more than looks. A herringbone sole suits most sandy courts, and running shoes slip. Our padel shoe finder tells you which sole to look for.

Is it a good workout?

An hour of padel burns roughly 400 to 600 kcal for an average adult. Work out your own number with the padel calories calculator.

Start with a lesson

One group lesson teaches the serve and the walls much faster than working it out alone.

Questions players ask

What do I need for my first padel game?

A racket, padel balls, court shoes with grip, comfy sports clothes and water. Many clubs rent rackets if you don't have one yet.

Can I use tennis balls for padel?

It's better not to. Padel balls have less pressure inside (about 11 psi against 14 psi for tennis, according to LTA Padel), so they bounce lower and play the way padel should.

How should I warm up for padel?

Spend 10 to 15 minutes on an easy jog, moving stretches, a few squats and lunges, then soft rallies, volleys, lobs and serves. Save long, held stretches for after you play.

Do I need special shoes for padel?

Tennis shoes are fine to start. Padel shoes with a herringbone sole grip sandy artificial grass best; running shoes slip and give little side support.

Do I need to own a racket to start padel?

No. Many clubs rent rackets, so you can try a few before you buy. When you do buy, a round shape with a medium weight is the easiest to start with.

What are the most important padel rules for beginners?

Serve underhand and diagonally, let the ball bounce once on your side before it hits your walls, make sure your shot lands on their ground before their walls, and don't touch the net.

Can you leave the court to play the ball in padel?

Only on courts built for out-of-court play and when the club allows it. Otherwise, a ball that bounces on your side and leaves the court is a point for the other team.
