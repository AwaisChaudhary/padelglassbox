How to volley in padel: technique, the right volley for each ball, and a fault finder.

Most points in padel are won at the net, and the volley is how you stay there. Learn the six basics, pick the right volley for the ball coming at you, then use the fault finder to fix the volley that keeps going wrong.

Quick answer

To volley in padel, keep the racket up and in front of you with a continental grip, turn your shoulders, and play a short, firm punch out in front of your body while stepping in with the opposite foot. Do not swing. Most volleys should go deep, past the service line, to keep your opponents back. Play a block on fast balls, a deep push on easy balls and a sliced volley on slow balls you can attack.

How do you hit a volley in padel?

Six basics. They are the same for forehand and backhand. Grip details in our grip guide.

Step 1

Racket up and in front

Wait with the racket head up, in front of your body and above net height. Then you only push it forward, you never have to lift it first.

Step 2

Continental grip

Hold it like a hammer, the same grip for forehand and backhand volleys. A flat, frying-pan grip opens your wrist and the ball flies up.

Step 3

Turn the shoulders

Turn your shoulders towards the side the ball is coming. The turn is your backswing, so the racket hardly goes back.

Step 4

Short, firm punch

A volley is a block or a short push, not a swing. Meet the ball out in front of your body and stop the racket soon after contact.

Step 5

Step in

Step towards the ball with the opposite foot (left foot on a right-hander's forehand, right foot on the backhand). The power comes from the step, not the arm.

Step 6

Low ball? Bend the knees

Get down to the ball with your legs and keep the racket head up. Do not drop the racket head to reach a low ball.

Types of padel volley

Pick by the ball, not by what you would like to play.

Block volley

For fast balls at you. Hold the racket firm and let the ball's own speed send it back. Aim deep and through the middle.

Push (deep) volley

For an easier rally ball. Push the ball deep, past the service line, to keep your opponents back.

Slice (fizz) volley

For slow balls you can attack. Cut under the ball for backspin so it stays low after the back glass and is hard to play.

Setup volley

A softer, sliced volley into the back corner, landing just before the glass. It sets up the next ball rather than trying to win now.

First volley

The first ball after you serve and run in. Get the racket up early, play it deep, and only finish if the ball is really easy.

Low volley

A ball below the net. Bend your knees, open the face a little and play it slow and deep. You cannot attack from below the net.

Where should you aim your volleys?

Deep, past the service line

A deep volley keeps the other pair back, where they cannot attack you. This is the default for most volleys.

Down the middle

The middle makes both opponents think about who takes it, and the net is lowest there. A slower ball down the middle dies near the back glass.

At the feet of a player coming in

If one opponent runs to the net, a low volley at their feet forces them to hit up to you.

Into the corner, only to finish

A firm volley into the back corner and the side glass (the double glass) can win the point, but only on an easy, high ball.

Volley drill, "push them back": one pair at the net, one at the back. The back pair starts each point. The net pair must land their volleys past the service line; after three good volleys, play the point out. Only the net pair can win the point; if the back pair wins it, they swap to the net. Questions players ask

How do you volley in padel?

Racket up and in front, continental grip, turn your shoulders and play a short, firm punch out in front of your body while stepping in with the opposite foot. Do not swing.

What grip is best for padel volleys?

The continental grip, the same one you serve with. It works for forehand and backhand volleys without changing grip at the net.

Where should I aim my volleys in padel?

Mostly deep, past the service line, and often down the middle. Aim at the feet of a player who is coming in, and go for the corners only to finish an easy ball.

Why does my volley go up in padel?

Usually the racket face is too open or you were late, so you met the ball behind you. Get the racket ready earlier, meet it in front and keep the face a little more closed.

How far from the net should I stand to volley?

About two to three metres, not touching distance. Too close and any lob goes over you; a small step back gives you time, and you step forward into each volley.

Should I swing on a padel volley?

No. A volley is a block or a short push. The power comes from your step and the ball's speed. A bigger swing only for a slow, high ball you can attack.
