Bandeja, víbora or smash: which overhead?

A lob is coming. Do you smash it, play a bandeja, try a víbora or let it go to the glass? Answer four quick questions and see the shot, how to hit it and where to aim, drawn on the court.

Quick answer

Smash only easy, high balls close to the net. Play a bandeja when you are further back or a bit off balance: slow, sliced and deep, so you keep the net. Play a víbora when you are balanced around the service line and want to attack: firmer, with side spin, into the side glass. If the lob will go over you, let it bounce off your back glass and lob back.

Bandeja

    What is the difference between a bandeja and a víbora?

    Bandeja

    Control, to keep the net

    Bandeja means tray in Spanish. It started as the answer to the lob: a way to stay at the net when a smash is too risky. It is slow, sliced and deep. Coaches call it a defensive shot.

    Víbora

    Spin, to attack

    Víbora means viper. It grew out of the bandeja but is hit harder, with side spin, so the ball skids off the side glass. It can win points, but it is riskier.

    Smash

    Power, to finish

    The smash is close to a tennis serve. It is the shot new players reach for most, but in padel it only pays on easy balls near the net. A hard smash from deep often bounces off the glass into the other pair's hands.

    Coaching guides describe the spin in slightly different words: some call bandeja spin backspin, others slice or underspin, and some say it can be flat. They all agree it is slower and more controlled than a víbora.

    When should you smash in padel?

    Yes: close and easy

    You are near the net, the ball is high and slow, and you are already under it. Hit down into the space between them.

    Careful: from the service line

    From here a flat smash often bounces off their back glass and comes back as an easy ball. A bandeja or víbora is usually the better pick.

    No: deep or moving back

    If you are near your own back glass, or running backwards, a smash is a guess. Play a bandeja, or let the ball go to the glass.

    Smashing out of the court

    Strong players sometimes smash so the ball bounces out over the side wall (often called por 3) or the back wall. That needs a lot of power and, on courts that allow it, the other pair may run out to play it. Read out-of-court play rules.

    How do you practise overheads?

    Bandeja to a target

    A partner feeds lobs from the other side. Every bandeja must land past their service line. Count how many in a row you can make.

    Bandeja, then back to the net

    After each bandeja, touch the net area with your racket before the next feed. It builds the habit of moving forward.

    One shot per session

    Spend 10 to 15 minutes on one overhead before mixing them. Filming yourself on a phone shows if you are turning sideways early enough.

    Questions players ask

    What is the difference between a bandeja and a víbora?

    A bandeja is a slow, sliced overhead hit deep to keep your place at the net. A víbora is firmer and hit with side spin into the side glass or corner to attack. The bandeja is safer; the víbora can win points but is riskier.

    When should I use a bandeja in padel?

    When you are lobbed at the net and the ball is not easy enough to smash, especially if you are further back or a little off balance. Hit it slow and deep, then move back to the net.

    When should I smash in padel?

    Only on an easy, high ball when you are close to the net. From the service line or deeper, a hard smash usually comes back off the glass and gives the other pair the attack.

    Is the bandeja a defensive shot?

    Yes. Coaches describe it as a defensive overhead. Its job is to keep your team at the net, not to win the point.

    Should beginners learn the bandeja or the víbora first?

    The bandeja. It is easier to learn, safer and used more often. Add the víbora once your bandeja is reliable.

    What grip do you use for a bandeja?

    The continental grip, the same one you use for volleys and serves. The víbora and smash use it too.

    What should I do if the lob goes over my head?

    Turn, follow the ball to your back glass, let it bounce and come off the glass, then lob it back deep. Running backwards to force an overhead is a common mistake.
