Padel equipment: what you need and what to wear.

Tell us how often you play, where, and what you already own. You get a packing list split into must bring, good to have and skip for now, with tips for each item. Tick things off as you pack.

Quick answer

To play padel you only need three things: a padel racket (many clubs rent them), padel balls (usually sold in cans of 3) and court shoes with grip, ideally padel shoes with a herringbone sole. Wear light sports clothes that wick sweat and let you move, with a pocket for the second ball. Most clubs have no strict dress code, but they expect sportswear and non-marking soles. A bag, overgrips and a protector can wait until you play every week.

What should you wear to play padel?

There is no special padel uniform. Tennis clothes work almost the same.

Top

A light T-shirt, polo or tank in a technical fabric (polyester or similar) that dries fast. Avoid heavy cotton: it gets wet and stays wet.

Bottoms

Shorts, a sports skirt with shorts under it, or leggings. They should stretch for lunges and low volleys, and not be too loose or too tight.

A pocket for the ball

You hold the second serve ball, so a deep, secure pocket (or ball clip) saves trips to the back of the court.

Socks

Sports socks with some padding, not thin casual socks. They help stop blisters when you slide and stop a lot.

Layers

A light top for the warm-up and after the game. In winter outdoors: a base layer, full-length leggings or joggers and a windproof jacket you can take off.

Sun and sweat

Outdoors in summer, wear a cap or visor and sports sunglasses against glare. Wristbands and a small towel help in any heat.

Is there a dress code for padel?

Most padel clubs have no strict dress code. They do expect sports clothes and shoes with non-marking soles that won't damage the turf. Some clubs and tournaments have their own rules, so check the club's website before your first booking.

Can you rent padel equipment?

Rackets

Many clubs rent rackets at reception or include them in beginner sessions. Ask when you book. Renting lets you try a few shapes before you buy.

Balls

Ask whether the club sells balls at the desk. Bringing your own fresh can is safer, because old balls bounce low.

Shoes

Ask the club whether it lends or rents shoes. For a first game, tennis or clay court shoes, or a borrowed pair of padel shoes, are fine.

Rental and prices differ from club to club. Check the club's own website or booking app.

Questions players ask

What equipment do you need for padel?

A padel racket, padel balls and court shoes with grip, plus sports clothes and water. Overgrips, a frame protector and a padel bag are useful later but not needed to start.

What do you wear to play padel?

Light sportswear that wicks sweat and stretches: a technical T-shirt or polo, shorts, a sports skirt or leggings, padded sports socks and court shoes. A pocket for the second ball helps.

Is there a dress code for padel?

Most clubs have no strict dress code, but they expect sports clothes and non-marking court shoes. Some clubs and tournaments set their own rules, so check before you go.

Can I play padel in running shoes?

It is better not to. Running shoes have little side support and the wrong grip for sand-filled turf. Use padel shoes, or tennis or clay court shoes for your first games.

Can I wear tennis clothes for padel?

Yes. Tennis clothes and padel clothes are almost the same: light, breathable and easy to move in.

Do I need to buy a racket to start padel?

No. Many clubs rent rackets. Rent or borrow for a few games, then buy a round, light, forgiving racket when you know you'll keep playing.

What should I wear for padel in winter?

Outdoors in the cold: a base layer, a light top, full-length leggings or joggers and a windproof jacket for the warm-up, with cushioned sports socks. Take layers off as you warm up.
