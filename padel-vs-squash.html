Padel vs squash: the differences, and how to switch.

Both use walls, so squash players pick up padel fast. But the walls work the other way round, the net changes everything, and padel is played in pairs. Here is the side-by-side, plus a fixer for the squash habits that cost padel points.

Quick answer

Padel is doubles over a net on a 20 x 10 m glass court, and the ball must bounce on your side before it hits your walls. Squash is mostly singles in a closed 9.75 x 6.4 m room with no net: you hit the ball onto the front wall above the 48 cm tin, and side and back walls can be used first. Padel uses tennis scoring; squash scores every rally, games to 11.

Your squash habit

Is padel easier than squash?

Easier to start

Padel's underarm serve, bigger court and slower ball mean rallies start in the first hour. Squash takes longer before you can keep the ball going.

Squash is more intense

Squash rallies rarely stop and you cover every ball yourself. Padel has short rallies and rest between points. See the padel calories calculator.

Both are hard to master

Padel's skill is in the walls, the lob and teamwork with your partner. Squash's skill is in length, width and moving your opponent. Your squash reading of the ball off the glass is a big head start.

Can squash players play padel?

What carries over

Reading a ball off the wall, quick feet, soft hands for drops and a compact swing. Most squash players rally well in their first game.

What to change

Let the ball come off your back glass, don't hit it into it. Lock your wrist. Share the court with a partner and move as a pair (where to stand).

First steps

Learn the wall rules, the serve rules and the grip, then follow the 30-day beginner plan.

Questions players ask

What is the difference between padel and squash?

Padel is doubles over a net on a 20 x 10 m glass court and the ball must bounce before it hits your walls. Squash is mostly singles in a closed 9.75 x 6.4 m room with no net, and you hit the ball onto the front wall.

Is padel easier than squash?

Most beginners find padel easier to start, thanks to the underarm serve, slower ball and doubles court. Squash is more tiring and takes longer to rally well.

Is padel good for squash players?

Yes. Squash players read the ball off the walls well and have quick hands. They mainly need to let the ball come off the back glass, lock the wrist and learn to play as a pair.

Do you hit the walls in padel like in squash?

No. In squash you can hit the ball into any wall on its way to the front wall. In padel your shot must land on the other side of the net before it touches their walls. You may play it off your own glass so it rebounds over the net, but you never use the walls to reach a front wall.

How is squash scored compared with padel?

Squash scores a point on every rally and games go to 11, win by 2. Padel uses tennis scoring: 15, 30, 40, game, with sets to 6 games.
