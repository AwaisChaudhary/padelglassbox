Padel racket for intermediate players: is it time to upgrade?

You can rally, you play every week and your first racket is starting to feel basic. Tell us what you play with now and how you play, and see which specs to keep, which to change and why.

Quick answer

Most intermediate players do well with a teardrop racket: medium balance, about 360 to 370 g, a soft or medium EVA core and a fibreglass or carbon face. Stay round and a little lighter if your arm gets sore or you play a steady game. Diamond rackets suit experienced attackers who play often and have no arm trouble. If your racket is not damaged and you play once a week or less, an upgrade will change less than lessons will.

    What changes from a beginner to an intermediate racket?

    Ranges are a guide. Racket guides draw the lines in slightly different places.

    BeginnerIntermediateAdvanced

    Sweet spotLarge, in the middleMedium, a little higherSmall and high on a diamond
    BalanceLow (head-light)MediumMedium to high

    Core feelSoftSoft or mediumMedium to firm
    FaceFibreglassFibreglass or carbonUsually carbon

    Why is teardrop the usual intermediate shape?

    Teardrop

    A step up, not a jump

    A teardrop sits between round and diamond. You get more punch on overheads than a round racket gives, while the sweet spot is still big enough to forgive a slightly late hit.

    Round

    Still a good choice

    Moving up a level does not mean you must leave round. If you win points with steady lobs and volleys, or your arm gets sore, a better round racket can be the right upgrade.

    Diamond

    Wait for it

    Most guides keep diamonds for advanced attacking players. The sweet spot is small and high, so mis-hits cost more, and the extra head weight puts more load on the elbow.

    Soft or hard: which core and face?

    Soft core

    Soft EVA or foam cushions the ball. It gives easier power at slow speeds, is kind to joints and is often suggested for intermediate players.

    Firm core

    Firm EVA feels stiff. It rewards fast, clean swings with control and power, but it forgives less when you miss the middle. Usually an advanced choice.

    Fibreglass face

    Softer and more flexible. Comfortable, forgiving and cheaper. Plenty of intermediate players stay with it.

    Carbon face

    Stiffer and more durable, with more precise feel, but more vibration reaches your arm. Rackets with carbon faces usually cost more.

    You will see numbers like 3K, 12K or 18K on carbon rackets. They describe the carbon weave. Brands use them differently, so compare how a racket feels rather than the number alone. See what 3K, 12K and 18K mean.

    How should I test a racket before I buy?

    Step 1

    Borrow or demo

    Ask friends at your club, or ask the club or shop for a demo racket. Play at least a full match with it.

    Step 2

    Test at the net

    Fast volleys show if it is too heavy or head-heavy. If you are late at the net, go lighter or lower balance.

    Step 3

    Test off the glass

    Defend balls off the back glass and lob. If balls keep flying long, the racket may be too powerful for you right now.

    Step 4

    Check your arm next day

    Any new elbow or wrist ache means the racket is too heavy, too head-heavy or too stiff for you.

    Questions players ask

    What padel racket should an intermediate player use?

    Usually a teardrop with medium balance, about 360 to 370 g, a soft or medium core and a fibreglass or carbon face. Steady players and anyone with a sore arm can stay with a round, lighter racket.

    When should I upgrade my padel racket?

    When it is damaged or feels dead, when you play two or more times a week and have outgrown it, or when it causes arm pain. If you play once a week or less and it is fine, keep it.

    Should an intermediate player use a diamond racket?

    Most racket guides keep diamond rackets for advanced attacking players. The small, high sweet spot forgives less, and the head weight loads the elbow. Try a teardrop first.

    How heavy should an intermediate padel racket be?

    About 360 to 370 g suits most intermediate players. Go to the lower end if your arm gets tired or sore.

    Is carbon better than fibreglass for intermediate players?

    Not always. Carbon is stiffer and more durable with a more precise feel, but sends more vibration to the arm. Fibreglass is softer and forgiving, and many intermediate players stay with it.

    Soft or hard core for an intermediate padel racket?

    Soft or medium. A soft core gives easier power and comfort; a firm core suits fast, clean swings and is usually for advanced players.

    Is a more expensive padel racket better?

    Not by itself. Higher prices often mean carbon and firmer cores, which suit advanced players. The right shape, weight and balance for your game matter more than the price.
