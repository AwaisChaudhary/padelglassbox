Padel rules quiz: how well do you know the rules?

Fifteen quick questions on the serve, the walls, the net and the scoring. Each answer comes with a short explanation and a link to the full rule, so you learn as you go. Good for beginners, club players and anyone who argues about calls.

Quick answer

The rules most players get wrong: the serve must bounce and be hit at or below the waist; a serve that bounces in the box and then hits the metal fence is a fault; your shot must bounce on their ground before it touches their glass; and you may play the ball into your own glass, but not your own fence. Test yourself with the 15-question quiz below.

Last updated 2026-11-27

Question 1 of 15

How must you hit the serve in padel?

The ball must bounce once on your side, behind the service line, and you hit it at or below your waist with at least one foot on the ground.

Question 2 of 15

Where must the serve land?

Like tennis, the serve goes diagonally into the box across the net. A ball on the line is good.

Question 3 of 15

Your serve bounces in the right box, then hits the metal fence. What is it?

After bouncing in the box, the serve may hit the glass, but not the metal mesh. Bounce then fence is a fault.

Question 4 of 15

Your serve clips the net and lands in the right box. What happens?

A net cord serve that lands in the right box (and doesn't then hit the fence) is a let. You replay that serve.

Question 5 of 15

Can the receiver volley the serve before it bounces?

The receiver must let the serve bounce. After the return, both teams may volley freely.

Question 6 of 15

Your shot crosses the net and hits their back glass before it bounces. Who wins the point?

Your shot must bounce on their ground first. Hitting their glass or fence before the bounce loses you the point.

Question 7 of 15

The ball bounces on your side, hits your back glass and comes out. Can you still play it?

After one bounce on your side the ball may hit your glass as often as it likes. You must hit it before the second bounce.

Question 8 of 15

Can you hit the ball into your own back glass to get it over the net?

Playing the ball into your own glass and back over the net is allowed and is a common way out of the corner.

Question 9 of 15

Can you hit the ball into your own metal fence to get it over?

Only your glass may be used to send the ball back. Hitting your own metal mesh loses the point.

Question 10 of 15

Your racket touches the net during the rally. What happens?

Touching the net, the posts or the other side's court with your body, clothes or racket while the ball is in play loses the point.

Question 11 of 15

The ball hits your partner before it bounces. Whose point is it?

If the ball touches you or your partner (except your racket) while it is in play, you lose the point.

Question 12 of 15

How many serve attempts do you get per point?

You get two tries. Miss both and you lose the point (a double fault).

Question 13 of 15

What is the golden point?

With the golden point there is no advantage: at deuce the next point wins the game. The receiving pair chooses which side receives.

Question 14 of 15

When is a tie-break played in a set?

At 6 games all you play a tie-break: first to 7 points, 2 points clear.

Question 15 of 15

Your opponents' shot bounces and flies out over the wall. Can you run out and play it back?

Out-of-court play is allowed by the rules when the court has open exits and the event allows it. Many clubs and courts do not, so agree before you play.

Questions players ask

What are the main rules of padel?

Serve underhand after one bounce, at or below the waist, diagonally into the box. The receiver lets it bounce. In the rally your shot must bounce on the other side before touching their walls. You may use your own glass but not your own fence. Scoring is like tennis.

Is a serve that hits the fence a fault in padel?

Yes, if it bounces in the service box and then hits the metal fence. If it bounces in the box and hits the glass, the serve is good.

Can you hit the ball off your own wall in padel?

Yes, off your own glass. You may hit the ball into your own glass so it goes back over the net. You may not hit it into your own metal fence.

Can you volley the serve in padel?

No. The receiver must let the serve bounce first. After the return, players can volley.

What happens if you touch the net in padel?

You lose the point, if the ball is still in play. That includes your racket, body and clothes.
