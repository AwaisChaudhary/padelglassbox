How to get better at padel: a level-by-level guide.

Pick your level and see the three things to work on next, which free tools to use, and how often to play.

Quick answer

To get better at padel: work on one thing at a time, play at least twice a week (one practice session with drills and one match), and track your errors to see what to fix. New players should learn the grip, a short swing and playing off the back glass. Improvers should volley 2 to 2.5 m from the net and learn the lob and bandeja. Club players should win the net with lobs and play patterns with their partner.

What level are you?

Not sure? Take the 2-minute level calculator.

Just started (under 6 months) (Level about 1 to 2.5)

Work on these three things next:

  1. Learn the continental grip and a short, compact swing.
  2. Let the ball come off the back glass instead of hitting it early.
  3. Get a reliable underarm serve in, every time.

  • Grip guide

How often: Play 1 to 2 times a week. One short lesson or a beginners' session every few weeks helps a lot.

Improver (6 months to 2 years) (Level about 2.5 to 3.5)

Work on these three things next:

  1. Play steady cross-court rallies at medium pace.
  2. Volley from about 2 to 2.5 m off the net with the racket up.
  3. Start using the lob, and learn the bandeja.

Use these:

  • Fix my padel
  • Where to stand
  • Bandeja guide
  • 4-week plan

How often: Two sessions a week: one practice (drills) and one match. Track one match a month.

Club player (Level about 3.5 to 4.5)

Work on these three things next:

  1. Win the net with lobs and soft balls at the feet.
  2. Make your bandeja deep and steady; add the víbora.
  3. Play patterns with your partner: who takes the middle, who covers the lob.

Use these:

  • Tactics trainer
  • Match tracker
  • Which side?
  • Drills planner

How often: Two to three sessions a week. Track matches to find your biggest leak and drill it.

Strong player (4.5 and up) (Level about 4.5 and up)

Work on these three things next:

  1. Out-of-court play and smashes por 3 or por 4 when they are on.
  2. Vary serve spin and target on purpose.
  3. Plan tactics by opponent: who to target and how.

How often: Three or more sessions a week, with fitness and recovery. Work with a coach on details.

How long does it take to get good at padel?

Padel is quick to start: most new players can rally in their first session, because the racket is short and the serve is underarm. Going from beginner to steady club level usually takes a year or two of playing twice a week. These are rough guides, not promises: lessons, fitness and playing other racket sports speed it up.

Questions players ask

How do I get better at padel?

Work on one thing at a time, play at least twice a week (one practice and one match), track your errors to find what to fix, and take a lesson now and then.

How long does it take to get good at padel?

Most people rally in their first session. Reaching steady club level usually takes a year or two of playing about twice a week, faster with lessons or a tennis or squash background.

What should a beginner padel player practise first?

The continental grip, a short swing, a reliable underarm serve, and letting the ball come off the back glass before hitting it.

How often should I play padel to improve?

Two sessions a week is a good start: one practice with drills and one match. Strong players add a third session plus fitness work.

Is it better to take padel lessons or just play?

Both. Matches teach decisions; lessons and drills fix technique faster than matches alone. A lesson every few weeks plus regular games works well for most players.
