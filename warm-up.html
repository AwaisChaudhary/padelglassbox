    How this warm-up works

    1. Get warm

    A couple of minutes of easy jogging raises your heart rate and sends blood to your muscles. Start slow.

    2. Move

    Moving stretches, like leg swings and lunges with a twist, loosen your hips, back and shoulders. Keep them moving: save long, held stretches for after the match.

    3. Activate

    Squats, lunges and short sprints wake up the legs you use for every split step and change of direction. LTA Padel's warm-up advice is built around exercises like these to help avoid hamstring, calf and thigh strains.

    4. Racket

    Start soft and close to the net, then add volleys, lobs and overheads. Build up the pace: your first smash should not be your hardest.

    5. Mind

    Agree three simple goals with your partner. It takes 30 seconds and helps you start the match switched on.

    After you play

    Walk for a couple of minutes, then gently hold stretches for your calves, thighs, hips, forearms and shoulders, about 20 to 30 seconds each.

    Short on court time?

    Do the first half before you go on

    The jog, moving stretches and squats need no court. Do them by the court while the last game finishes, then use your court time for the racket steps.

    Playing early or in the cold?

    Choose the 15 minute version. Cold muscles need longer, and the balls feel flatter too. Read when to change padel balls.

    Something hurts?

    A warm-up is not a fix for an injury. If you feel sharp pain, stop and get advice from a doctor or physiotherapist.

    Questions players ask

    How long should a padel warm-up be?

    About 10 to 15 minutes is enough for most players. LTA Padel suggests 10 to 15 minutes, starting with a short jog, then exercises, then a few minutes of rallying.

    Should I stretch before padel?

    Use moving stretches before you play, such as leg swings, arm circles and lunges with a twist. Keep long, held stretches for after the match.

    What should a padel warm-up include?

    A short jog, moving stretches, a few leg exercises such as squats and lunges, short sprints, and then racket work: soft rallies, volleys, lobs, easy overheads and a few serves.

    Can I warm up without a court?

    Yes. The jog, moving stretches, squats, lunges and sprints can all be done next to the court before your booking starts.

    Does the timer work without internet?

    Once the page has loaded, the timer runs in your browser. Keep the screen on, because some phones pause timers when the screen locks.
