How to play off the back glass in padel.

Letting the ball come off the back glass is the skill tennis players find hardest when they start padel. Learn the five basics, then use the rebound picker: tell it what the ball does off the glass and it tells you where to stand and which shot to play.

Quick answer

To play off the back glass in padel, turn side-on as the ball passes you, move away from the glass with small steps, watch the ball hit the wall and let it finish its rebound, then bend your knees and step forward into the shot. Fast, flat balls come out fast and far, so give them more room; slow, high balls die near the glass, so move closer. From the back, a high lob or a low ball at the net player's feet is usually best; a high rebound can be attacked with a bajada.

How do you play the ball off the back glass?

Five basics for forehand and backhand.

Step 1

Turn side-on early

As soon as the ball passes you, turn sideways to the side wall and take the racket back. Do not face the glass square on.

Step 2

Give it room

Move away from the glass with small, quick steps. Most mistakes come from standing too close to the wall.

Step 3

Watch the ball into the glass

Keep your eyes on the ball as it hits the glass. The first contact tells you where it will come out.

Step 4

Let it come back to you

Wait for the ball to finish its rebound. It is now moving towards the net, so it needs little power from you.

Step 5

Low and step in

Bend your knees, step forward into the shot and swing up through the ball, before it bounces again.

How to read the rebound

Fast and flat

Comes off the glass fast and low and travels a long way back towards the net. Stand further from the wall.

Slow and high

A lob loses speed in the glass and drops close to the wall. Move in closer, or it bounces twice before you get there.

Topspin

Kicks up and forward off the glass. Expect it higher and further out.

Slice (backspin)

Stays low after the glass. Get down early and lift it.

Side glass, then back glass

Usually comes out towards the middle of the court, nearer your partner. Agree who takes these.

Back glass, then side glass

Runs further along the side wall and comes out nearer the net, on the same side. Follow it along the wall.

Common back glass mistakes

Crowding the glass

The ball comes out further than you think. Start further from the wall than feels right.

Hitting too early

Taking the ball before it has finished its rebound gives a rushed, weak shot. Wait.

Too much power

The ball is already coming towards the net. A smooth swing is enough; hard hits fly out or into the net.

Back to the court

Turning your back on the court means you lose track of the other pair. Stay side-on.

Flat feet

Backing up flat-footed leaves you stuck. Stay on the balls of your feet and keep moving.

Back glass drill: a partner feeds slow, deep balls past the service line. First let each ball bounce twice (court, glass, court) before you hit, then take away the extra bounce. Mix forehand and backhand feeds. Next level: the feeder sends waist-high rebounds and you drive them low, then move to the net. Build a full session in the drills planner.

Questions players ask

How do you play off the back wall in padel?

Turn side-on, move away from the glass, watch the ball into the wall and let it finish its rebound, then bend your knees and step into the shot. It is already coming towards the net, so use a smooth swing.

How far from the back glass should I stand?

Further than you think for fast, flat balls, which come out a long way. Closer for slow, high lobs, which drop near the wall. Reading the speed is the key.

What shot should I play off the back glass?

Most of the time a high lob over the net pair, or a low ball at their feet. If the ball comes off high, at waist to shoulder height, you can attack with a bajada.

What is a bajada in padel?

An attacking shot played on a ball that comes off the back glass high. You take it on the way down, at about shoulder height, and drive it low and hard, then move to the net.

Why do I keep missing balls off the back glass?

Usually you stand too close to the glass, face the wall square on or hit too early. Turn side-on, give the ball more room and wait for it.

Should I let the ball hit the back glass or take it before?

Let balls that land past the service line come off the glass. The exception is a soft ball that will die right at the wall: take that one before it reaches the glass.
