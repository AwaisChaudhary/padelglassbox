Quick answer

Most beginners do best with a round padel racket: low balance (under about 260 mm), a weight of about 345 to 360 g, a soft foam core and a fibreglass face. Improving players often move to a teardrop shape, and experienced attacking players to a diamond. If your elbow or shoulder gets sore, stay light, low-balance and soft.

What are the 3 padel racket shapes?

Most control

Round

The sweet spot is large and right in the middle, and the weight sits towards the handle. Forgiving, easy to handle, and the usual first racket. Plenty of top players use them too.

All-round

Teardrop

A mix of round and diamond, with medium balance. More punch than a round racket while still being fairly easy to control.

Most power

Diamond

Weight sits towards the head and the sweet spot is smaller and higher up. Big smashes, but less forgiving, so it suits experienced attackers.

What do padel racket specs mean?

Weight

Most adult rackets weigh roughly 345 to 385 g. Heavier rackets hit harder but tire your arm sooner; lighter ones are quicker at the net. New players usually do best under about 360 g.

Balance

Measured in mm from the bottom of the handle. Low balance (under about 260 mm) means control and quick hands. High balance (over about 270 mm) means power but more strain. Decode and compare two rackets.

Core foam

Soft foam gives easy power and comfort at slow speeds. Hard foam gives control when you swing fast.

Face material

Fiberglass is softer and forgiving. Carbon is stiffer and more precise, and usually costs more. Decode any racket label.

How do you choose a grip and overgrip?

The cheapest upgrade you can make to any racket. How to hold it: padel grip guide. Looking after the rest of it: racket care.

Grip vs overgrip

The base grip is the thick, cushioned layer that comes on the handle. An overgrip is a thin, light tape you wrap over it. It protects the base grip, which is harder to replace, and soaks up sweat.

When to change it

Change it when it looks dirty or worn, or when it feels slippery, wet or less sticky. Social players often get a few sessions from one. Players who sweat a lot, or play in heat, change it far more often.

How to put one on

Take off the old overgrip. Start at the bottom of the handle, by the butt cap, and wrap up towards the head, keeping each turn parallel and slightly overlapping. Cut the end straight and fix it with the finishing tape.

Handle feels too thin?

Padel handles often feel small in bigger hands. Overlap the overgrip more as you wrap, or add a second overgrip, to make the handle thicker. Overlap less for a thinner feel. Use the finger test in the grip guide to check.

Questions players ask

How do I hold a padel racket?

Use the continental grip: stand the racket on its edge and shake hands with the handle, so the V between your thumb and first finger sits on the top edge. It works for almost every shot.

What padel racket should a beginner buy?

Most beginners do best with a round racket: low balance, a lighter weight, soft foam and a fiberglass face. It's forgiving and easy to control.

Round, teardrop or diamond: which is best?

Round gives the most control, diamond the most power, and teardrop sits in between. Many players start round, move to teardrop, then try diamond.

How heavy should a padel racket be?

Most adult rackets weigh around 345 to 385 g. Pick the lighter end if you're new or have sore elbows, and the heavier end if you hit hard.

What does the balance number on a padel racket mean?

It is the distance in millimetres from the bottom of the handle to the point where the racket balances. Under about 260 mm is head-light and easy to control; over about 270 mm is head-heavy and more powerful.

How often should I change my padel overgrip?

When it starts to feel slippery or less sticky, or looks dirty and worn. For social players that is often every few sessions; players who sweat a lot change it much more often.

How do I make my padel racket handle thicker?

Overlap the overgrip more as you wrap it, or put a second overgrip on top of the first.

Does a racket help with tennis elbow?

A lighter racket with a soft core and low balance puts less strain on your arm. If pain continues, see a doctor or physio.
