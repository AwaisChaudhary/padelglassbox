The 3 racket shapes

Most control

Round

The sweet spot is large and right in the middle, and the weight sits towards the handle. Forgiving, easy to handle, and the usual first racket. Plenty of top players use them too.

All-round

Teardrop

A mix of round and diamond, with medium balance. More punch than a round racket while still being fairly easy to control.

Most power

Diamond

Weight sits towards the head and the sweet spot is smaller and higher up. Big smashes, but less forgiving, so it suits experienced attackers.

What the specs mean

Weight

Most adult rackets weigh roughly 350 to 385 g. Heavier rackets hit harder but tire your arm sooner; lighter ones are quicker at the net.

Balance

Low balance (towards the handle) means control and quick hands. High balance (towards the head) means power but more strain.

Core foam

Soft foam gives easy power and comfort at slow speeds. Hard foam gives control when you swing fast.

Face material

Fiberglass is softer and forgiving. Carbon is stiffer and more precise, and usually costs more.

Questions players ask

What padel racket should a beginner buy?

Most beginners do best with a round racket: low balance, a lighter weight, soft foam and a fiberglass face. It's forgiving and easy to control.

Round, teardrop or diamond: which is best?

Round gives the most control, diamond the most power, and teardrop sits in between. Many players start round, move to teardrop, then try diamond.

How heavy should a padel racket be?

Most adult rackets weigh around 350 to 385 g. Pick the lighter end if you're new or have sore elbows, and the heavier end if you hit hard.

Does a racket help with tennis elbow?

A lighter racket with a soft core and low balance puts less strain on your arm. If pain continues, see a doctor or physio.
