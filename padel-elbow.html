Padel elbow: why it happens and the arm-load check.

Pain on the outside of the elbow is the most common padel complaint. Answer eight questions about your racket, your shots and how much you play. The check shows which habits load your arm most and what to change first.

Quick answer

Padel elbow is the padel name for tennis elbow (lateral epicondylitis): pain on the outside of the elbow from overloaded forearm tendons. In padel it is most often linked to late contact and wristy backhands, gripping too tight, mishits, a racket that is heavy, head-heavy or stiff for you, and playing much more than before. For arm-friendly gear, look at about 340 to 360 g, low balance (under about 260 mm) and a soft core. The NHS advises rest from what hurts and seeing a GP if it is no better after 2 weeks.

    What causes padel elbow?

    Usually several small things together, not one shot.

    Late contact

    Hitting the ball beside or behind your body, often after it comes off the back glass, makes the forearm do the work instead of your legs and shoulders.

    Wristy backhands

    Short backhands where the wrist flicks over the ball load the tendons on the outside of the elbow. Turn your shoulders and keep the wrist firm.

    Gripping too tight

    Squeezing the handle all the time keeps the forearm muscles tense on every shot. Hold it at about 3 or 4 out of 10 between shots.

    Off-centre hits

    Mishits send more vibration into the wrist and elbow. This is common while technique is new.

    A racket that is heavy for you

    A heavy or head-heavy (high balance) racket is more weight to swing and stop on every shot.

    A very stiff racket

    A hard core and stiff face pass on more shock. Softer foam cores absorb more of it.

    Too much, too soon

    Going from one game a week to four, or long sessions without rest days, gives the tendons no time to adapt.

    What is the best padel racket for tennis elbow?

    No racket cures it. Shop and coach guides broadly agree on these starting points; it makes sense, but studies have not proven it.

    How do you stop padel elbow coming back?

    Meet the ball in front

    Move your feet so you hit in front of your body. Off the back glass, step back and let the ball come to you. See playing off the back glass.

    Turn for backhands

    Use your shoulders and a firm wrist rather than a flick. Check the continental grip in the grip guide.

    Warm up the forearm

    Wrist circles, light band work and easy rallies before you hit hard. Use the warm-up timer; in winter take longer: cold-weather padel.

    Strengthen slowly

    Forearm strength work, such as slow wrist-extensor exercises with a light band or weight 2 to 3 times a week, is widely recommended. Ask a physio which exercises suit you.

    Build up gradually

    Add one session a week at a time and keep rest days. A training plan helps you spread the load.

    Use fresh balls

    Dead, flat or damp balls need more effort to hit. See when to change balls.

    How do you treat padel elbow?

    Rest from what hurts

    The NHS advises resting from the movements that make it worse. Playing through pain can slow recovery.

    At home

    NHS self-care includes painkillers such as paracetamol or an anti-inflammatory gel, and a hot or cold pack wrapped in a towel for up to 20 minutes every 2 to 3 hours. A forearm strap may help.

    See a GP or physio

    If it is no better after 2 weeks of self-care, see a GP. A physiotherapist can give a tailored exercise plan. It often improves within weeks, but it can last much longer.

    Coming back

    Start with short, easy sessions, a lighter or softer racket if yours is heavy, and stop if the pain returns. A brace eases symptoms but does not fix the cause.

    Questions players ask

    What is padel elbow?

    Padel elbow is the padel name for tennis elbow (lateral epicondylitis): pain on the outside of the elbow caused by overloaded forearm tendons from repeated gripping and wrist movement.

    What is the best padel racket for tennis elbow?

    Look for a racket that is light but not extreme (about 340 to 360 g), low balance (under about 260 mm), a round shape for a bigger sweet spot, and a soft core. No racket cures it, so fix technique and rest too.

    Can I play padel with tennis elbow?

    It is better to rest from what makes it worse. The NHS advises rest, painkillers or gel and hot or cold packs, and seeing a GP if it is no better after 2 weeks. Come back with short, easy sessions.

    Does a heavy padel racket cause tennis elbow?

    A racket that is heavy or head-heavy for you adds load on every swing, so it is a common factor. But a racket that is far too light can make you swing harder. Choose one you can swing smoothly.

    How long does padel elbow last?

    It often gets better within a few weeks of rest, but the NHS says tennis elbow can last much longer, sometimes more than a year. Coming back too fast is a common reason it returns.

    Does an elbow brace help padel elbow?

    A forearm strap or brace may ease the pain while you play, but it does not fix the cause. Combine it with rest, better technique and arm-friendly gear.
