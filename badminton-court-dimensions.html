Badminton court dimensions

The full badminton court with every line explained: singles and doubles, service courts, net height and the space you need above and around the court.

Quick answer

A badminton court is 13.40 m long and 6.10 m wide for doubles (44 × 20 ft), and 13.40 × 5.18 m for singles (44 × 17 ft). The short service line is 1.98 m from the net, the doubles long service line is 0.76 m inside the back line, and the net is 1.524 m (5 ft) high in the middle and 1.55 m at the posts.

Singles vs doubles lines

Singles uses the inner sidelines and the full length to the back line, for serving and rallies. Doubles uses the outer sidelines, but when serving the shuttle must land short of the doubles long service line, 0.76 m in from the back. After the serve, the whole doubles court is in play.

Space around and above

Leave at least 2 m beyond the back lines and about 1.5 m at the sides, and as much ceiling height as you can: international halls use at least 12 m over the court, but 7 to 9 m is common in club and school halls. Low ceilings mostly hurt high serves and clears.

Same size as pickleball

A badminton doubles court and a pickleball court are both about 44 × 20 ft, so sports halls often share the floor. The lines and net heights differ: see our pickleball court dimensions.

Questions players ask

What are the dimensions of a badminton court?

13.40 m long and 6.10 m wide for doubles (44 × 20 ft); singles is 13.40 × 5.18 m (44 × 17 ft).

How high is a badminton net?

1.524 m (5 ft) at the centre and 1.55 m (5 ft 1 in) at the posts.

How far is the short service line from the net?

1.98 m (6 ft 6 in) on each side.

What is the doubles long service line?

A line 0.76 m inside the back boundary. In doubles the serve must land in front of it; after the serve the full length is in play.

How much ceiling height does badminton need?

International events use at least 12 m over the court; club halls are often 7 to 9 m. More height is better for high serves and clears.
