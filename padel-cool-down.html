Padel cool-down: a stretch timer for after you play.

Pick 5, 10 or 15 minutes and press start. The timer walks you through each stretch with a beep at every change, so you can do it on the bench with your phone next to you. Then read why it helps, how long to hold a stretch, and what to do if you are sore the next day.

Quick answer

A good padel cool-down takes 5 to 15 minutes: 2 minutes of easy walking, then slow, held stretches for the calves, thighs, hips, lower back, shoulder and forearm. Hold each stretch for about 20 to 30 seconds, gently, without bouncing and never into pain. Soreness that peaks a day or two after a hard or first game is normal and usually fades within a few days.

    Should you cool down after padel?

    Yes, if you can spare five minutes. Padel is stop-start: sprints, lunges, twists and overheads, often late in the evening. A short walk lets your heart rate and breathing come down slowly, and held stretches after play are the right time to work on the hips, legs and shoulder you used. Padel New Zealand, the national federation, lists a cool-down with stretching as part of its injury-prevention advice, next to a proper warm-up.

    Step 1

    1. Walk it off

    Two minutes of easy walking. Don't sit down straight after the last point, especially after a long match.

    Step 2

    2. Stretch, slowly

    Held stretches for the muscles padel uses most: calves, front and back of the thigh, hips, lower back, shoulder and forearm.

    Step 3

    3. Drink and refuel

    Drink water, more after a hot or long session, and eat a normal meal with some protein and carbohydrate within a few hours.

    How long should you hold a stretch?

    About 20 to 30 seconds for each stretch is a good target. The American College of Sports Medicine's guidance for adults is to hold static stretches for 10 to 30 seconds, gently, to the point of mild tightness, not pain. Breathe out as you settle into the stretch, keep still and don't bounce. Once or twice per side is enough after a match.

    When 1

    Before play: moving

    Leg swings, lunges with a twist and short sprints. Save long, held stretches for later. Use the warm-up timer.

    When 2

    After play: held

    Slow, held stretches while your muscles are still warm. This is what the timer on this page does.

    When 3

    Rest days: either

    A 10-minute stretch on a rest day is fine and can help with stiffness. Keep it gentle.

    Why am I so sore after padel?

    New players and anyone back after a break often feel sore one or two days after playing, mostly in the thighs, calves, bottom and shoulder. This is called delayed onset muscle soreness (DOMS). The American College of Sports Medicine describes it as soreness that usually starts 12 to 24 hours after unfamiliar or hard exercise, peaks at about 24 to 72 hours, and fades within about 5 to 7 days. Padel's lunges, low volleys and quick changes of direction are new movements for most people.

    Questions players ask

    How long should a padel cool-down be?

    About 5 to 15 minutes: 2 minutes of easy walking, then held stretches for the legs, hips, lower back, shoulder and forearm.

    Should you stretch before or after padel?

    Do moving stretches (leg swings, lunges, twists) before you play, and slow, held stretches after you play, when your muscles are warm.

    How long should I hold each stretch?

    About 20 to 30 seconds. Sports-medicine guidance for adults is 10 to 30 seconds, to mild tightness, not pain, without bouncing.

    Is it normal to be sore after playing padel?

    Yes, especially after your first games or a long break. Muscle soreness usually peaks one to two days after playing and fades within a few days. Sharp, one-sided or worsening pain is different: get it checked.

    What muscles does padel work?

    Mostly the calves, thighs, bottom and hips from lunges and side steps, the trunk from twisting, and the shoulder and forearm of your hitting arm.

    What should I do the day after padel if I am sore?

    Keep moving gently: a walk, an easy bike ride or a 10-minute stretch. Drink water, sleep well, and play lighter until it eases.
