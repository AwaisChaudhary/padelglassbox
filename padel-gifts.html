Padel gifts: what to buy a padel player.

Answer six quick questions about the person and your budget. You get a short list of padel gifts that suit them, what to check before you buy, and the gifts to avoid. Then print a free gift voucher for a lesson, a court or a night of padel.

Quick answer

The safest padel gifts are things every player uses up: a pack of padel balls, overgrips and frame protector tape (small budget), a padel bag, a court booking or a lesson (medium budget), and padel shoes in their size (bigger budget). Only buy a racket if you know the shape and weight they like, or if they don't own one yet. Otherwise give a voucher and let them choose.

    Is a padel racket a good gift?

    Yes

    For someone who has none

    A new player who rents rackets will use a simple first racket a lot. Pick a round shape, light weight, soft core and low balance. See cheap padel rackets for what to look for. For a child, use the junior size finder.

    Maybe

    If you know their racket

    If you know the shape, weight and balance they like, or they have already played with a model, a racket can be a great gift. Keep the receipt.

    Avoid

    A surprise upgrade

    Regular players are picky. A racket that is too heavy, too head-heavy or too stiff can feel wrong and even hurt their arm. Give a voucher and let them choose, or send them to the upgrade check.

    To find out what suits them without spoiling the surprise, ask what shape their racket is and how heavy it feels, or try our racket finder with what you know.

    Which small padel gifts do players really use?

    Padel balls

    Balls lose their bounce after a few sessions, so players always need more. Buy padel balls, not tennis balls: padel balls have less pressure inside. More on when balls are dead.

    Overgrips

    A thin wrap that goes over the handle. It gets slippery and worn, and HEAD suggests changing it at least every two months. A pack of 3 is a cheap, useful gift. See how to put one on.

    Frame protector tape

    A strip that goes round the top of the frame and stops chips when the racket hits the glass or floor. Single protectors cost about $9 to $12 in the US (Padel Browser, Sep 2026). It adds a few grams at the top, so some players skip it. More in racket care.

    Racket cover or ball saver

    A cover keeps sand, heat and knocks off the racket. A ball saver tube keeps opened balls lively a little longer.

    Questions players ask

    What is a good gift for a padel player?

    Things they use up: padel balls, overgrips and frame protector tape for a small budget; a bag, a court booking or a lesson for a medium budget; padel shoes in their size for a bigger budget.

    Should I buy a padel racket as a gift?

    Only if they don't have one yet, or you know the shape, weight and balance they like. Regular players are picky about rackets, so a voucher is often the better gift.

    What is a cheap padel gift?

    A can of padel balls, a pack of overgrips, frame protector tape, sweatbands or padel socks. All cost under about 20 in pounds, dollars or euros.

    What padel gift can I give a beginner?

    A simple first racket (round, light, soft) if they rent one now, a lesson with a coach, padel balls, or padel shoes with a herringbone sole.

    How many balls are in a can of padel balls?

    Most cans or tubes of padel balls hold 3 balls. Shops also sell multi-packs of several cans.

    Can I give padel lessons as a gift?

    Yes. Ask the club or coach whether they sell gift vouchers, or print our free voucher and book the lesson together.

    Are padel shoes a good gift?

    Yes, if you know their size in that brand and choose the right sole, usually herringbone. Keep the gift receipt in case they need another size.
