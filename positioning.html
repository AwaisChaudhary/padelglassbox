Where to stand in padel.

Pick a moment in the point and see where all four players should be. Your team is at the bottom: you on the left, your partner on the right.

Quick answer

At the net, stand about 2 to 2.5 metres from the net. When you defend, stand about 1 metre in front of the back glass. Move up and back with your partner as a pair, about 3 metres apart, and both slide towards the side the ball is on. Avoid one up and one back: it opens a gap down the diagonal.

What are the habits of good padel positioning?

Move as a pair

Imagine a rope of about 3 metres between you and your partner. When one moves up, back or across, the other follows. Gaps open when you move on your own.

Win the net

Most points are won at the net. Stand about 2 to 2.5 metres from it, close enough to volley and far enough back to reach a lob.

Give the glass room

When you defend, stand about 1 metre in front of the back glass. Too close and the ball comes off the glass into your body. Too far forward and lobs beat you.

Shade with the ball

Both players slide towards the side the ball is on. The player nearer the ball covers the line, the other covers the middle.

Agree on the middle

Balls down the middle cause the most mix-ups. By default the player with the forehand in the middle takes them. Call "mine" or "yours" early.

Reset after a lob

If a lob goes over you, both players go back together, play a safe shot, then look for the next chance to move forward again.

Should I play on the left or the right side in padel?

Right side

The drive side

For a right-handed player the forehand is on the outside, near the side wall. This player plays more steady, controlled balls and builds the point.

Left side

The revés side

For a right-handed player the forehand is in the middle. This player takes many middle balls and more of the high balls and smashes, so it is often the more attacking role.

Mixed hands

One right-hander, one left-hander

Many pairs put both forehands in the middle: the left-hander on the right side and the right-hander on the left. Try both ways and see what feels natural.

Questions players ask

Where should I stand at the net in padel?

About 2 to 2.5 metres from the net, slightly towards your side of the centre line. The player nearer the ball can be a little closer, with the partner half a step back to cover the middle and the lob.

How far from the back glass should I stand?

About 1 metre in front of it when you defend or receive. That gives the ball room to come off the glass so you can play it comfortably.

Why is one up and one back bad in padel?

It leaves a big diagonal gap between the two players, and opponents can hit through it or at the feet of the net player. Move up and back together instead.

Who takes the ball down the middle?

As a default, the player with the forehand in the middle. For two right-handers that is the player on the left. Whoever calls it first should take it.

Where does the server's partner stand?

Usually at the net, about 2 metres from it, on the other half of the court. The rules let the server's partner stand anywhere on their side, but a serve that hits them is a fault.

Should I play on the left or the right side?

For right-handers, the left side suits players who like to attack and smash, and the right side suits steady players who like control. Both are important.

Where should I stand to receive a serve in padel?

Near the back of your half, about 1 metre in front of the back glass and a little towards the side wall. Most serves bounce and reach the glass, so give the ball room.

Should both padel players be at the net?

Yes, when you can. Most points are won at the net, so move forward together after a good lob or a deep ball, and go back together when you are lobbed.
