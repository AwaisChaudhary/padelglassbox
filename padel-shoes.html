Padel shoes: which sole do you need?

Grip is the first thing to get right. Answer four questions about your courts and your game and see which sole pattern to look for, how much cushioning, and when to replace your shoes.

Quick answer

For most padel courts (artificial grass with sand), look for a herringbone (zig-zag) sole. On low-sand or carpet-style courts, an omni or hybrid sole grips better. Don't play in running shoes. If you play every week, plan on new shoes about every 4 to 6 months.

What are the types of padel shoe sole?

Herringbone

A zig-zag pattern from toe to heel. It is the classic padel sole: the grooves let sand pass through, so you can slide and stop on sandy artificial grass. It has the most grip of the three.

Best for sandy courts

Omni

Lots of small rubber studs. It grips well on courts with little sand, but on a very sandy court it can feel like you are on ice skates.

Best for low-sand courts

Hybrid

Zig-zag on the outside, where you push off when you change direction, and studs or a softer pattern in the middle. A good choice if you play at different clubs.

Good on most courts

How should padel shoes fit?

Snug, not tight

Your heel should not lift and your foot should not slide when you stop. Tight shoes give blisters.

Room for your toes

Your foot moves forward when you stop hard. Leave a little space, about a thumb's width, in front of your longest toe.

Check the width too

Some models are narrow. If the sides press on your foot, try a wider model, not just a bigger size.

Try them with your padel socks

Shop later in the day, when feet are a little bigger, and wear the socks you play in.

Comfortable from day one

Padel shoes should feel good the first time. Wear new shoes for a short session before a long match.

Pain that stays

Shoes help, but they don't fix injuries. If pain in your feet, knees or ankles doesn't go away, see a doctor or physio.

Questions players ask

Do you need special shoes for padel?

You can try padel in tennis shoes, but padel shoes grip the sandy artificial grass better and support quick stops and turns. Don't play in running shoes: they slip and give little side support.

What sole is best for padel shoes?

Herringbone (a zig-zag pattern) for courts with lots of sand. Omni (small studs) or a hybrid sole for courts with little or no sand.

Can I use tennis shoes for padel?

Yes, to start. They grip less well on padel turf and are made for different movement, so if you play every week, padel shoes are a good buy.

Can I play padel in running shoes?

It's best not to. Running shoes are made for moving forward, so they have a smooth sole and little side support. That makes it easier to slip or roll an ankle on court.

How long do padel shoes last?

If you play every week, about 4 to 6 months. If you play now and then, often a year or more. Replace them when the sole is worn smooth or the cushioning feels hard.

Should padel shoes be tight?

Snug but not tight. Your heel shouldn't lift and your foot shouldn't slide, but leave a little room in front of your toes because your foot moves forward when you stop.

What is the difference between omni and herringbone soles?

Herringbone has zig-zag grooves that let sand pass, so it grips on sandy courts. Omni has many small studs and grips better on courts with little sand.
