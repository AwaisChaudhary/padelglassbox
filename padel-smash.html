How to smash in padel, and when not to.

The smash is the shot everyone wants to hit and the one that loses club players the most points. Use the smash picker to see if this lob is a smash, a bandeja, a víbora or one to let off the glass, then learn the technique and what the x3 and x4 smashes are.

Quick answer

To smash in padel, turn side-on as soon as you see the lob, move back with side steps, point at the ball with your free hand and hit it high and a little in front of your head with a continental grip, then move forward to the net again. Only smash short lobs you can hit above your head while moving forward; for deeper lobs, play a bandeja or víbora. A flat smash stays in the court and comes back off the glass. An x3 (por tres) bounces and goes out over the 3 m side wall; an x4 goes out over the back wall.

How do you hit a smash in padel?

Six steps. Same grip as your volley: see the grip guide.

Step 1

Turn side-on at once

As soon as you see the lob, turn your shoulders so your non-hitting shoulder points at the net. Turning first is what gives you time.

Step 2

Move with crossover steps

Go back with side or crossover steps, not by running backwards. Get behind the ball so you can hit it while moving forward.

Step 3

Point at the ball

Lift your free hand and point at the ball. Hold the racket up behind your head, ready, with a continental grip.

Step 4

Hit high and in front

Meet the ball at the highest point you can, a little in front of your head. A ball that drops low in front of you should be a bandeja, not a smash.

Step 5

Snap and finish across

Let the wrist and forearm turn over at contact for speed and topspin, and finish the racket across your body.

Step 6

Get back to the net

After the smash, move forward again at once. A good pair will often get the ball back off the glass.

Types of padel smash: flat, kick, x3 and x4

From the most common to the hardest.

Flat smash

A hard, flat hit aimed down the middle or at the feet. It does not leave the court, so it comes back off the back glass if the other pair waits for it. Hit it at an angle or at feet, not straight into the glass at speed.

Kick smash

Heavy topspin so the ball bounces deep and kicks up high off the back glass. It keeps the other pair back and buys you time, rather than winning the point at once.

x3 (por tres)

A topspin smash that bounces in the other court, hits the back glass and flies out over the side wall, which is 3 m high. That is where the name comes from. It needs a lot of power and a short lob.

x4 (por cuatro)

A smash that bounces and goes out over the back wall (glass plus mesh, about 4 m in total). It is the hardest version. The names x3 and x4 are informal, and descriptions differ a little between sources.

What is an x3 smash in padel, and how do you hit one?

Pick the right ball

Only a comfortable, short lob that you can hit at full stretch. The closer you are to the net, the easier it is. From further back you need much more topspin.

Aim for the bounce

Aim to bounce the ball about a metre before the other pair's service line, so it hits the back glass high and near the corner and flies out over the side wall. Cross-court is easier than down the line.

Jump and brush up

Jump, hit as high as you can with the face a little open, and brush up and over the ball for topspin, finishing across your hip.

It is not always a winner

On courts with door openings and space outside, the other pair may run out and play the ball back before it bounces again. Rules: out-of-court play.

Common smash mistakes

Smashing every lob

Most lobs at club level are too deep to smash. Play a bandeja or a víbora and keep the net instead.

Hitting too hard

A very hard flat smash into the back glass comes back fast and gives the other pair an easy ball. Placement beats speed.

Ball too low

Waiting until the ball drops to head height, then trying to smash. Hit high, or change to a bandeja.

Running backwards

Backpedalling face-on is slow and you lose balance. Turn first, then use side steps.

Forgetting your partner

Call the ball ('mine' or 'yours'). On a lob down the middle, the player on the forehand side usually takes it.

Smash drill, "call it": a partner feeds lobs of different depths. Before contact, call out "smash", "bandeja" or "víbora", then play that shot. After each one, run back to the net and touch it with your racket. Start with 10 short lobs, then mix in deep ones. Build a full session in the drills planner. Questions players ask

How do you smash in padel?

Turn side-on early, move back with side steps, point at the ball, hit it high and slightly in front of your head with a continental grip, snap the wrist over and finish across your body. Then move back to the net.

When should you smash in padel?

Only on short lobs you can hit well above your head while moving forward or standing still. For deep lobs, or when you are moving backwards, play a bandeja or a víbora.

What is an x3 in padel?

An x3 (por tres) is a topspin smash that bounces in the other court, hits the back glass and goes out over the 3 m side wall. It is very hard to return unless the court allows players to run outside.

What is an x4 in padel?

An x4 (por cuatro) is a smash that bounces and goes out over the back wall, which is glass plus mesh, about 4 m high. It is the hardest smash to hit.

Why does my smash come back in padel?

A flat smash stays inside the court, so the ball hits the back glass and comes back for the other pair. Aim at their feet or at an angle, or use topspin, instead of hitting straight and hard into the glass.

Bandeja or smash: which is better?

Neither is better; they do different jobs. The bandeja keeps you at the net safely on a deeper lob. The smash is for short, easy lobs you can finish.
