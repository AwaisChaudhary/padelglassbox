Padel training plan generator: your next 4 weeks.

Pick your level, how often you play, how long and your goal. Get a 4-week plan with a warm-up, drills, game play and reps for every session. Print it or copy it to your phone.

Quick answer

A good padel training plan for amateurs is 2 or 3 sessions a week, with a rest day between hard sessions. Each session: 10 minutes of warm-up, 20 to 40 minutes of drills on one main goal, then game play to test it, and 5 minutes to cool down. Keep the same goal for 4 weeks: learn it (week 1), repeat it and count (week 2), add pressure (week 3), and test it in matches (week 4).

Your 4-week plan

    How often should I train padel?

    Rules of thumb for amateurs. Your body and your diary decide in the end.

    Sessions a weekWhat to expectGood split
    1Keeps your level; slow progressOne mixed session: 1 drill on your goal, then games
    2Steady progress for most players1 drill session + 1 match session
    3Fast progress if you rest well2 drill sessions + 1 match session, not on 3 days in a row
    4For keen club and strong players2 drill sessions, 1 mixed, 1 match; take at least 1 full rest day a week

    What should a padel training session look like?

    Warm-up: 10 min

    Raise the heart rate, move the joints, then soft rallies. Use the padel warm-up timer.

    Drills: 20 to 40 min

    One or two drills on your main goal. Count something (a streak, targets hit) so you can see progress week by week.

    Game play: 15 to 40 min

    Play points or games where your goal matters, for example "only points won at the net count double".

    Cool-down: 5 min

    Walk, easy stretches for calves, hips, shoulders and forearms, and drink water.

    Why a 4-week plan?

    Four weeks is long enough to turn a new habit into a reliable shot, and short enough to stay motivated. Each week has a job:

    1. Week 1, learn: slow, easy feeds; get the movement right.
    2. Week 2, repeat: the same drills, now counting your streak or targets.
    3. Week 3, pressure: faster feeds, moving first, or points that count.
    4. Week 4, test: fewer drills, more match play; log a match with the tracker and compare.

    Then pick the next goal and build a new plan.

    Questions players ask

    How many times a week should I play padel to improve?

    Two or three times a week is enough for steady progress for most amateurs, with a rest day between hard sessions. One session a week keeps your level but progress is slow.

    What should a padel training session include?

    A 10-minute warm-up, 20 to 40 minutes of drills on one goal, game play that tests that goal, and a 5-minute cool-down.

    How long does it take to get better at padel?

    Most players see a clear change in one shot after about 4 weeks of focused practice, 2 to 3 sessions a week. Moving up a full level usually takes months.

    Can I train padel on my own?

    Yes. Back-glass rallies, tap-ups, volleys against a wall, serve targets and footwork all work alone. Choose 'On my own' in the plan generator.

    Should beginners follow a padel training plan?

    A simple one helps: one main goal, like keeping the ball in play, and mostly game play. Choose 'New' and the plan keeps drills short and easy.
