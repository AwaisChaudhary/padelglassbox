Padel court surfaces: what you are playing on, and how it plays.

Most padel courts are artificial grass with sand, but not all grass is the same. Answer four quick questions about the court you play on and the checker tells you what surface it probably is, how the ball will bounce, which shoe sole grips best and one tactic that works on it.

Quick answer

The official rules allow three kinds of padel court floor: artificial grass, other synthetic surfaces, and porous concrete or cement. Almost every club court today is artificial grass (10 to 15 mm pile) dressed with fine, rounded silica sand. More sand and shorter, straight monofilament fibres make a slower court with a lower bounce; less sand and taller, split fibrillated fibres play faster and livelier. The floor must be one colour: green, blue or terracotta (black is allowed indoors only).

What surface is a padel court made of?

The FIP rules say the floor can be porous concrete or cement, a synthetic material, or artificial grass, as long as the ball bounces evenly and water does not collect on it. Artificial and synthetic grass should meet the Spanish UNE standard for padel surfaces. In practice nearly every new club court is sand-dressed artificial grass laid on a porous base. In the UK, LTA Padel's guidance asks for a single-colour, sand-dressed synthetic turf with monofilament as the preferred fibre.

Why does the sand matter?

Sand 1

It holds the fibres up

Fine, rounded silica sand fills the grass so the fibres stand up and the ball bounces the same everywhere. Sharp sand cuts the fibres, so courts use rounded grains.

Sand 2

More sand = slower

A freshly topped-up court with lots of sand plays slow and low, and the ball can skid. Feet slide more until the sand settles.

Sand 3

Less sand = faster

When sand is thin, or pushed into the corners by wind and play, the ball comes through quicker and higher, and bare patches feel slippery.

Clubs brush the court to spread the sand evenly (LTA Padel guidance says weekly) and top it up when it gets thin. If one side of the court plays differently from the other, uneven sand is the usual reason.

What colour is a padel court?

The FIP rules allow green, blue or terracotta, in one even colour that stands out clearly from the walls. A black floor is allowed only indoors. The lines are 5 cm wide and are usually white. Outdoor courts fade in strong sun, which is normal and does not change the rules. All the court measurements are on the padel court dimensions page.

How long does a padel court surface last?

LTA Padel tells UK venues to plan to replace the surface about every 5 years, depending on how busy the court is, and to brush it every week. Installer and industry guides give a similar 4 to 6 years, with a deep clean once or twice a year. Signs a court needs work:

Sign 1

Flat, shiny fibres

The grass lies down in the busy areas (behind the service lines and at the net) and the ball skids or dies.

Sign 2

Bald or lifting patches

You can see the backing, or seams lift. These spots are slippery and can catch a foot.

Sign 3

Odd bounces

The ball bounces differently in different areas, often because the sand is uneven or packed hard.

Tell the club if you see a lifting seam or a puddle that does not drain: they are safety issues, not just playing issues. Building or pricing a court? See the padel court cost calculator.

Questions players ask

What surface is a padel court?

Usually artificial grass dressed with fine silica sand. The official rules also allow other synthetic surfaces and porous concrete or cement.

Why is there sand on a padel court?

The sand holds the grass fibres up, gives an even bounce and lets rain drain. More sand makes the court slower and the bounce lower.

What is the difference between monofilament and fibrillated padel grass?

Monofilament has separate straight fibres and plays slower with a more even bounce; it lasts longer. Fibrillated has flat, split ribbons, plays a bit faster and wears out sooner.

What colour should a padel court be?

Green, blue or terracotta, in one even colour that contrasts with the walls. Black is allowed only on indoor courts.

Are indoor padel courts faster than outdoor courts?

Often a little, because they stay dry and the balls stay lively, but the surface and the amount of sand matter more than the roof.

How often should a padel court be resurfaced?

About every 5 years on a busy court, according to LTA Padel guidance, with weekly brushing and sand top-ups in between.

What shoes are best for a sandy padel court?

Padel shoes with a herringbone (zig-zag) sole. On courts with little sand, an omni or hybrid sole grips better.
