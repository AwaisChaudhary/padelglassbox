Quick answer

Good padel drills for beginners are rallies with a target (keep the ball going for 10, then 20 shots), back-glass feeds, soft volleys at the net and serves at targets. Practise one skill at a time, warm up first, and count something in every drill so you can see progress. A 30-minute session can be a 5-minute warm-up, four 5-minute drills and a short game.

    Back-glass rally on your own

    On your own

    Stand about two metres from the back glass. Hit the ball softly into the glass, let it come back and bounce once, then hit it again. Mix forehands and backhands.

    Count: Best streak without a miss. Start by aiming for 20, then 50.

    Corner rebound chase

    On your own

    From a back corner, hit the ball at an angle into the glass so it comes back across the court. Move to it, play it, and send it back to the same corner. Work for 90 seconds, then rest for 30.

    Count: Balls played in 90 seconds.

    Racket tap-ups

    On your own

    Tap the ball straight up off the racket, keeping it at about head height. To make it harder, turn the racket over and use the other face on every tap. You can do this at home.

    Count: Best streak of taps.

    Volleys against a wall

    On your own

    Find a solid wall outside the court. Stand two or three metres away and volley the ball against it without letting it bounce. Keep the racket up in front of you and use short punches, not big swings.

    Count: Best streak of volleys.

    Serve targets

    On your ownWith a partnerFour players

    Put four targets in the service box, such as cones, bottles or spare balls: two near the centre line and two near the side glass. Serve ten balls at each one, then serve from the other side. With a partner, they return each serve and you swap after 20.

    Count: Serves that hit or land close to the target, out of 40.

    Footwork and split step

    On your ownWith a partnerFour players

    No ball needed. Do short side steps along the baseline, quick in-and-out steps on a chalk grid or agility ladder, and a small hop (the split step) each time you imagine the opponent hitting. Stay on your toes.

    Ladder rallies

    With a partnerFour players

    Rally on half the court. First keep the ball going for 2 shots, then 3, then 4, and so on. A miss sends you both back to 2. To make it harder, one player stays at the net and the other plays from the back.

    Count: Highest rung you reach together.

    Back-glass feeds

    With a partnerFour players

    Stand on the same side of the net. The feeder throws the ball onto the floor just in front of the back glass. The player lets it come off the glass and plays it softly back into the feeder's hands. Swap after ten. Harder: feed into the corner so the ball hits two walls.

    Count: Clean returns out of ten.

    Cross-court rally

    With a partnerFour players

    Both players hit only diagonal, cross-court shots from the back of the court, at a steady pace. Count the rally as a team.

    Count: Best team rally. Aim for 30, then 50.

    Volley to volley

    With a partnerFour players

    Both players stand at the net, two or three metres apart, and keep soft volleys going between you. No winners, just control.

    Count: Best team streak of volleys.

    One up, one back

    With a partnerFour players

    One player at the net volleys calmly to a player at the back, who lets some balls come off the back glass before returning them. Swap roles halfway through.

    Count: Longest rally.

    Lob and overhead rotation

    With a partnerFour players

    One player lobs from the back. The other moves back and plays a controlled bandeja, then a víbora or smash when the bandeja is reliable. After each overhead, step back in towards the net. Swap roles after 15 lobs.

    Count: Overheads that land in, out of 15.

    Yellow card, red card

    With a partnerFour players

    Play normal games, one against one on half a court or two against two on the full court. Your first mistake in a game is a yellow card. A second mistake in the same game is a red card, and you lose that game.

    Count: Games won.

    How do you plan a padel practice session?

    Warm up first

    Start with a few minutes of easy movement and soft hitting before any drill. Our warm-up timer gives you a 10 or 15 minute routine.

    One skill at a time

    LTA Padel advises working on one skill per session instead of everything at once. Pick one focus above and most of the session goes to it.

    Control before power

    Hit at a steady pace and aim for the target. New players can aim for about 20 clean shots in a row before they add movement or speed.

    Count something

    A streak, a score out of 40 or a rung on the ladder. Numbers show you are getting better and make drills more fun. Log them in the drill list.

    Finish with a game

    End with a game that rewards fewer mistakes, like yellow card, red card. It puts the drill work under a bit of match pressure.

    Swap roles often

    In partner drills, change who feeds and who hits every few minutes, so both players practise and nobody gets cold.

    Questions players ask

    What are good padel drills for beginners?

    Rallies with a target streak, back-glass feeds where a partner throws the ball at the glass for you, soft volleys at the net, serves at targets and racket tap-ups at home. Keep them slow and count your best streak.

    How long should a padel practice session be?

    20 to 60 minutes works. Even 20 minutes a week on top of your matches helps. Spend a few minutes warming up, most of the time on one skill, and finish with a short game.

    Can you practise padel on your own?

    Yes. You can rally against the back glass, chase rebounds from the corners, serve at targets, volley against a wall and tap the ball up on your racket at home.

    What padel drills can two players do?

    Ladder rallies on half court, cross-court rallies, volley to volley at the net, back-glass feeds, one up one back, lobs and overheads, and the yellow card, red card game.

    How do you practise playing off the back glass?

    Have a partner throw the ball onto the floor just in front of the back glass. Let it come off the glass, then play it softly back. When that is easy, feed into the corner so it hits two walls.

    What is the yellow card, red card padel drill?

    A game where your first mistake in a game is a yellow card and your second is a red card, which loses you that game. It rewards keeping the ball in play.
