Best padel racket for beginners: check any racket in 30 seconds.

Found a racket in a shop or online? Put in its shape, weight, balance, core and face, and see if it suits a beginner, spec by spec, with the reason. No brands pushed, no sign-up.

Quick answer

The best padel racket for a beginner is round, about 355 to 370 g, head-light (low balance), with a soft EVA or foam core and a fibreglass or fibreglass-carbon face. That mix gives a big sweet spot, easy depth and less strain on the arm. You don't need to spend much: good beginner rackets start just under £50 or about $60 to $130.

    What should a beginner padel racket have?

    The same advice comes up in most beginner buying guides from padel shops and coaches. These are general ranges, not rules.

    SpecBeginner pickWhy
    ShapeRoundThe sweet spot sits low and in the middle, so off-centre hits still go over. Teardrop is fine once you hit cleanly; diamond is for power players.
    WeightAbout 355 to 370 g (adults); 340 to 360 g for smaller players or a sore armLight enough to swing late without tiring, heavy enough to stay stable on volleys.
    BalanceLow or evenA head-light racket is quick at the net and kinder on the elbow.
    CoreSoft EVA or foamSoft cores give easy depth with a slow swing and soak up vibration.

    What are the most common mistakes when buying a first padel racket?

    Buying a pro's racket

    Diamond, head-heavy, hard carbon rackets are built for players who already hit cleanly and fast. For a beginner they feel dead on mis-hits and can hurt the elbow.

    Paying for power you can't use

    Expensive usually means stiffer. At the start, control and comfort win more points than power. Our cheap padel rackets guide shows what good budget rackets look like.

    Too heavy "for stability"

    A racket over about 375 g gets slow and tiring by the third set. If your arm aches after playing, go lighter and softer (see padel injuries).

    Ignoring the grip

    A thin handle makes you squeeze too hard. Add an overgrip or two to build it up, and learn the continental grip.

    When should a beginner upgrade?

    Most players keep a beginner racket for their first year or so. Think about a change when you hit the sweet spot most of the time, play the bandeja on purpose and want more power on overheads.     Questions players ask

    What is the best padel racket for a beginner?

    A round racket of about 355 to 370 g with a low balance, a soft EVA or foam core and a fibreglass face. It forgives off-centre hits and is kind to the arm.

    What weight padel racket should a beginner use?

    Most adult beginners do well with about 355 to 370 g. Smaller players, teens or anyone with elbow pain can go to about 340 to 360 g.

    Is a carbon padel racket good for beginners?

    A carbon-fibreglass mix is fine. Full high-K carbon faces are stiffer and suit fast swings, so most beginners get more from fibreglass.

    How much should a beginner spend on a padel racket?

    Not much. Good beginner rackets start just under £50 in the UK and about $60 to $130 in the US. Many clubs also hire rackets while you decide.

    What padel racket should a tennis player buy?

    A teardrop or round racket of about 350 to 365 g with a low or medium balance and a soft or medium core. Avoid diamond, head-heavy rackets in your first season: they reward the long tennis swing you need to shorten.

    Is a heavier padel racket better for tennis players?

    Not at first. Tennis players often like weight, but around 360 g is plenty while you learn quick net play. Go heavier later if you want more power.

    Round, teardrop or diamond for beginners?

    Round. It has the biggest, lowest sweet spot. Teardrop is the usual second racket; diamond is for advanced, attacking players.
