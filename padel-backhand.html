How to hit a padel backhand (one or two hands)

The backhand is where most padel players lose points, because opponents aim at it. Learn the four steps, decide between one and two hands, and use the fault fixer to find out why your backhand floats, hits the net or comes late.

Quick answer

To hit a padel backhand, hold a continental grip, turn your shoulders sideways early with your free hand on the throat of the racket, and step across with your front foot. Keep the backswing short, meet the ball slightly in front of your front foot with a firm wrist and a slightly closed face, and finish towards your target. Most padel players hit one-handed because it reaches further and works better off the glass; two hands give more stability on balls close to the body.

    How do you hit a backhand in padel, step by step?

    Step 1

    Continental grip, free hand on the throat

    Use the same continental grip as for your forehand and volleys, so you never change grip in a fast rally. Keep your free hand on the throat of the racket as you turn: it helps you turn the shoulders and keeps the backswing short.

    Step 2

    Turn sideways early

    As soon as you see the ball coming to your backhand, turn your shoulders and step across with your front foot (the right foot for a right-hander). Bend your knees and stay low.

    Step 3

    Short backswing, contact in front

    Take the racket back no higher than your shoulder. Meet the ball a little in front of your front foot, at the side of your body, with a firm wrist.

    Step 4

    Slightly closed face, finish to target

    Keep the face slightly closed so the ball stays low over the net, then finish smoothly towards your target. Do not slow the racket down at contact.

    One-handed or two-handed backhand in padel?

    Most padel players hit one-handed. Two hands is allowed and some good players use it.

    One-handedTwo-handed
    ReachMore: you can stretch for wide ballsLess: you need to move closer to the ball
    Off the back glassEasier, often with sliceHarder when the ball comes off the glass close to you
    At the netNatural for volleys and blocksSlower for fast volleys
    Balls at body heightHarder to control with paceMore stable, more power
    Contact pointFurther in frontA little further back, closer to the body
    Good forMost players, especially from the startTennis players with a two-handed backhand, players who lack wrist strength

    A common mix: two hands when you have time and the ball is close, one hand when you have to reach or play off the back wall. Volleys and blocks at the net are almost always one-handed.

    How do you play a backhand off the back glass?

    Step 1

    Read the bounce

    If the ball bounces after the service line, let it hit the back glass. Follow it all the way to the wall.

    Step 2

    Turn early, move away

    Turn sideways as soon as it passes you and take small steps towards the net, so the ball comes back to the side of your body.

    Step 3

    Let it drop to waist height

    Do not hit it on the way up. Wait until it drops to about waist height, then play a short, controlled swing.

    Step 4

    Aim deep and safe

    Play it back deep or cross-court, or lob if they are at the net. The glass ball is about getting back into the point, not winning it.

    What are the most common padel backhand mistakes?

    Mistake 1

    Late contact

    The racket goes back only when the ball arrives, so you hit it next to or behind your body. Turn as soon as you know it is a backhand.

    Mistake 2

    Open face, ball floats

    A face tilted up sends the ball high, which gives the net pair an easy volley or smash. Close the face a little.

    Mistake 3

    Reaching instead of moving

    Stretching with the arm, not the feet, kills control and strains the elbow. Take small steps so the ball is at the side of you.

    Mistake 4

    Slowing down at contact

    Fear of hitting long makes players stop the racket at contact. Keep a smooth, full but short swing and control the ball with the face.

    Which drills improve a padel backhand?

    Drill 1

    Cross-court backhand rally

    Two players on the backhand diagonal rally cross-court backhands only. Count how many you can keep low and deep in a row.

    Drill 2

    Wall forehand-backhand

    Against a wall or the side glass, alternate forehand and backhand. It trains the early turn and the grip you keep for both.

    Drill 3

    Glass rebound feeds

    A partner feeds balls into your back glass on the backhand side. Turn, move, let it drop, play it deep. 10 balls, then swap.

    Questions players ask

    How do you hit a backhand in padel?

    Use a continental grip, turn sideways early with your free hand on the racket throat, step across with the front foot, keep the backswing short and meet the ball slightly in front of you with a firm wrist. Finish towards your target.

    Should I use a one-handed or two-handed backhand in padel?

    Most players use one hand because it reaches further and is easier off the glass and at the net. Two hands give more stability on balls close to the body, so some tennis players keep it for those.

    What grip do you use for a padel backhand?

    The continental grip, the same as for the forehand, volleys and serve. That way you never need to change grip in a fast rally.

    Why does my padel backhand float?

    Usually the racket face is open (tilted up) or you hit late, under the ball. Turn earlier, meet the ball in front and close the face a little.

    How do you hit a backhand off the back glass?

    Let the ball hit the glass, turn sideways early, move with it towards the net and wait until it drops to about waist height. Then play a short, controlled swing deep or cross-court.

    Is a two-handed backhand allowed in padel?

    Yes. The rules do not limit how many hands you use. It is just less common because padel rewards reach and quick volleys.
