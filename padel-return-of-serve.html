How to return serve in padel: where to stand and which return to play.

Half your points start with a return. Most club players lose them by trying too much. Use the return picker to choose a safe, smart return for the serve you face, and fix the five most common return problems.

Quick answer

To return serve in padel, stand about one step behind the service line and close to (not against) the side glass, take the racket back as soon as you see the serve's direction, and let a good serve come off the side glass before you hit. The goal is not a winner: return deep or low at the feet of the server coming to the net, or lob over the net player. You must let the serve bounce once in your box; you may not volley it.

Where should you stand to return serve in padel?

Just behind the service line

Start about one small step behind the service line, facing the server, not turned sideways. Too far back and you rush in late; too close and good serves jam you.

Near the side glass, not on it

Stand close enough to the side glass to cover a serve into it, with room to swing. Against a left-hander, or on the backhand side, the ball hugs the wall more: move a little closer.

On your toes

Split step as the server hits, read the side, and turn. The racket goes back before the ball bounces.

Your partner

Your partner usually stands back too, about level with you, ready to defend. Formation choices: where to stand on court.

Five common return problems and the fix

Going for winners

The return's job is to get the ball back and make the server's team play another shot. Aim deep or at the feet of the server coming in.

Taking it too early

Against a good serve, let the ball come off the side glass. That gives you room to swing and better timing.

Moving towards the glass

After the bounce, step away from the glass, not into it, so the ball can come to you.

Racket back too late

See which side the serve goes, then take the racket back at once, before the bounce. Then only your feet need to adjust.

Not reading the server

Most servers repeat their spin and target. After a few points, start a step towards where their serve usually goes.

Return of serve rules in padel

The short version. Full detail and a checker: serve rules.

Must it bounce?Yes. The serve must bounce in your service box before you hit it. Volleying the serve loses the point.
Which player returns?The player on the side the serve goes to. The returning pair keeps the same sides for the whole set and can only swap at the start of a new set.
Serve hits the fenceIf the serve touches the wire fence before you hit it, it is a fault. If it hits the glass after bouncing in the box, it is in play.
Net touchA serve that clips the net and lands in the box is a let: serve again.
Golden pointAt 40-40 with golden point, the returning pair chooses which side receives. See golden point vs star point.

Questions players ask

Where do you stand to return serve in padel?

About one step behind the service line, facing the server, near but not against the side glass. Adjust a little after a few serves once you see what the server does.

Can you volley a serve in padel?

No. The serve must bounce in your service box first. If you volley it, you lose the point.

What is the best return of serve in padel?

The safe one. A deep or low return at the feet of the server coming in, or a lob over the net player when the serve is hard. Save winners for slow, easy serves.

Should I hit the return before or after the glass?

Against a good serve into the side glass, let it come off the glass first: you get more room and better timing. Take an easy, central serve early and be more aggressive.

How do I return a serve with side spin?

Give it more space. A side-spin serve moves away from the glass towards you after the bounce, so stand a little further from the glass and let it come in.

How do I return a low, sliced serve?

Stand a little closer to the glass and bend your knees early, because the ball stays low after the glass. Play it slow and deep or lob; do not try to hit it hard.
