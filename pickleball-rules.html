Pickleball rules, explained simply

How to serve, the two-bounce rule, the kitchen and how scoring works, with a quick quiz to check you've got it.

Quick answer

Serve underhand and diagonally, so the ball clears the kitchen. The receiving side must let the serve bounce, and the serving side must let the return bounce (the two-bounce rule); after that you can volley. Never volley while standing in the 7 ft non-volley zone, the kitchen. Games are usually to 11, win by 2, and in traditional scoring only the serving side scores.

Last updated 2026-11-25

1. The serve

Serve underhand, from behind the baseline, diagonally into the opposite service court. The paddle must meet the ball below your waist with an upward arc (the volley serve), or you may drop the ball and hit it after it bounces (the drop serve). The serve must land beyond the kitchen line; a serve on the kitchen line is a fault. You get one serve, no second serve.

2. The two-bounce rule

The return of serve must bounce, and then the serving side's next shot must bounce too. After those two bounces, either side can volley (hit the ball in the air) or play it off the bounce.

3. The kitchen

The non-volley zone is the 7 ft area on each side of the net. You can't volley while touching it, including the line, and you can't let your momentum carry you into it after a volley. You can step in any time to play a ball that has bounced.

4. Faults

A rally ends when the ball lands out, goes into the net, bounces twice, is volleyed from the kitchen, or a player touches the net. Balls landing on any line are in, except a serve on the kitchen line.

5. Scoring (traditional)

Only the serving side scores. Games go to 11, win by 2. In doubles both partners serve before the serve passes to the other team, except at the start, when only one player serves; that's why the first score is called "0-0-2". Some leagues and events also use rally scoring, where every rally wins a point. Keep track with our pickleball score keeper.

6. Calling the score

In doubles, call three numbers before serving: your score, the opponents' score, then 1 or 2 for which server you are. In singles, call two numbers, and serve from the right when your score is even and the left when it's odd.

Quick quiz: is it legal?

Pickleball vs padel

Pickleball has no walls, an underhand serve that must clear the kitchen, and side-out scoring to 11. Padel uses tennis scoring and the glass walls are in play. See padel vs tennis vs pickleball.

20 × 44 ft with a 7 ft kitchen each side: see the pickleball court dimensions.

Questions players ask

What is the two-bounce rule in pickleball?

The serve must bounce before it's returned, and the return must bounce before the serving side hits it. After that, players can volley.

What is the kitchen in pickleball?

The non-volley zone, 7 ft from the net on each side. You can't volley while touching it or carry into it after a volley.

Can you go in the kitchen in pickleball?

Yes, any time, as long as you don't volley there. Stepping in to play a ball that has bounced is legal.

How do you score in pickleball?

In traditional scoring only the serving side scores. Games go to 11, win by 2. Some events use rally scoring, where every rally scores.

Why do you say 0-0-2 at the start?

In doubles only one player on the first serving team serves at the start, so the game begins as if that player were the second server.
