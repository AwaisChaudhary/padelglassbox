The bars are a rough guide worked out from weight and balance only. The core foam, face material, thickness and how stiff the racket is also change how it feels, so try a racket before you buy when you can.

Balance: low, medium or high

Every padel racket has more weight above its middle point.

BalanceRough balance pointHow it feelsOften found on
Low (head-light)Under about 260 mmQuick to move, easy to control, gentler on the arm. Less free power.Round rackets
Medium (even)About 260 to 270 mmA mix of control and power.Teardrop rackets
High (head-heavy)Over about 270 mmMore weight behind smashes and hard hits. Slower at the net and more load on the elbow and shoulder.Diamond rackets

Shops and brands draw these lines in slightly different places, so treat them as a guide. A racket is 45.5 cm long at most, so its middle is 227.5 mm from the bottom. Most rackets balance well above that, between about 250 and 285 mm.

Shape is only a clue

Round rackets are usually head-light and diamond rackets head-heavy, but not always. Some round rackets are quite head-heavy, and some diamonds are fairly even. The balance figure tells you more than the shape.

Shape moves the sweet spot

The sweet spot is the part of the face that gives the cleanest hit. On a round racket it is big and in the middle. On a teardrop it sits a little higher. On a diamond it is smaller and near the top.

Balance changes the swing

Two rackets with the same weight can feel very different. The one with the weight further up feels heavier when you swing it, because more of the weight is far from your hand.

Weight: what the grams mean

WeightRangeTends to suit
LightUnder about 355 gNew players, juniors, players with sore elbows or shoulders, and anyone who wants fast hands at the net.
MediumAbout 355 to 370 gMost improving players. A good mix of feel and power.
HeavyOver about 370 gStrong, experienced players who hit hard and want more power and stability.

Most adult rackets weigh between about 345 and 385 g. Weights on the box can be a few grams out, and many are given as a range, such as 360 to 375 g.

Find your racket's balance at home

You need a table, a pencil and a tape measure.

Step 1

Set it up as you play

Leave the overgrip, frame protector and any tape on the racket. They all change the balance a little.

Step 2

Find the level point

Lay a round pencil on a table. Put the racket across it, face flat, and slide it slowly until it rests level without tipping either way.

Step 3

Measure

Mark the spot where the pencil touches. Measure from the very bottom of the handle to that mark in millimetres. That number is the balance point.

Weight

Weigh it too

A kitchen scale works. Weigh the racket as you play with it, then put both numbers into the decoder above.

Small changes that move the balance

Adding an overgrip

An overgrip adds a few grams at the handle, so the balance moves a little towards your hand and the racket feels a touch lighter to swing. Two overgrips move it more. See our grip and overgrip tips.

Frame protector tape

Tape around the top of the frame protects it from scrapes on the floor and walls, but it adds weight at the head. The balance moves up and the racket feels heavier to swing.

Lead or weight tape

Some players add thin weight tape to tune a racket. Put it near the top for more power, or near the handle for more control. The rules allow added weights if they are a sensible size and position.

What the rules say about the racket

From the FIP Rules of Padel, in force from 1 January 2026.

PartLimit
Total length45.5 cm at most
Handle20 cm long at most, 50 mm wide and 50 mm thick at most
Head26 cm wide and 38 mm thick at most (a small 2.5% allowance when measuring thickness)
HolesAny number. In the main area each round hole is 9 to 13 mm across
FacesBoth must be flat. They can be smooth or rough
Wrist cordMust be fixed to the handle, not stretchy, 35 cm long at most, and worn on your wrist
Weight and balanceNo limit. You can choose any weight and balance

If your cord breaks or you drop the racket during a point, your pair loses that point. More in our padel rules guide.

Questions players ask

What is a good balance for a padel racket?

For beginners and players who want control, a low balance of under about 260 mm from the bottom of the handle. For a mix of control and power, about 260 to 270 mm. Hard hitters who want power often choose over about 270 mm.

How is padel racket balance measured?

It is the distance in millimetres from the bottom of the handle to the point where the racket balances level. Rest it across a pencil, slide it until it sits level, and measure to that point.

What does head-heavy mean on a padel racket?

More of the weight sits towards the top of the head. That adds power to smashes and hard shots, but the racket is slower to move and puts more load on your elbow and shoulder.

How heavy should my padel racket be?

Most adult rackets weigh about 345 to 385 g. New players and those with sore arms usually do best under about 360 g; strong, experienced players often choose 365 to 375 g or more.

Is a lighter padel racket better for tennis elbow?

A lighter racket with a low balance usually puts less strain on the arm, and a soft core helps too. If the pain continues, see a doctor or physio.

Does an overgrip change the balance?

A little. It adds weight at the handle, so the balance point moves towards your hand. Frame protector tape does the opposite and moves it towards the head.

Is there a weight limit for padel rackets?

No. The FIP rules limit the size of the racket, 45.5 cm long, 26 cm wide and 38 mm thick at most, but not its weight or balance.
