Fix my padel: why do I keep doing that?

Pick the problem that costs you the most points. Get the likely cause in plain words, three fixes, and one drill with reps to practise this week.

Quick answer

Most padel problems for new and improving players come from four habits: swinging like in tennis, standing in the wrong place (too close to the net or the back glass), trying to win the point from the back, and not talking to your partner. Shorten your swing, volley about 2 to 2.5 m from the net, wait about 1 m off the back glass, and call every middle ball.

      Why does my padel ball keep hitting the back glass?

      Likely causes:

      • A long tennis-style swing that sends the ball flat and fast.
      • Trying to win the point from the back of the court.
      • Hitting the ball late, so the racket face opens and the ball flies up.

      Three fixes:

      1. Take the racket back only to shoulder height and finish short, in front of you.
      2. Aim about 1 m over the net at medium pace. From the back, your job is to keep the ball in play, not to win.
      3. Meet the ball in front of your body with a firm wrist and a slightly open face (a gentle slice).

      Drill: Cross-court control rally. Rally cross-court with a partner at medium pace. Count balls in a row that bounce before the service line on the other side and do not hit the back glass on the full. Reps: 3 sets of 20 in a row.

      How do I play the ball off the back glass in padel?

      Likely causes:

      • Standing too close to the glass, so there is no room when the ball bounces out.
      • Turning late and facing the glass instead of the side wall.
      • Hitting the ball while it is still rising off the glass.

      Three fixes:

      1. Wait about 1 m in front of the back glass, not against it.
      2. Turn your shoulders to the side wall as soon as you see the ball will reach the glass.
      3. Let the ball come off the glass and drop to about waist height, then hit it forward and up over the net.

      Drill: Throw, catch, hit. A partner throws the ball so it bounces and hits the back glass. First catch it as it comes off (10 times), then hit it instead. Reps: 10 catches, then 3 sets of 10 hits.

      Why do I lose points at the net in padel?

      Likely causes:

      • Standing too close to the net, so any lob goes over you.
      • The racket hangs low between shots, so you are late on fast balls.
      • Trying to hit every volley hard.

      Three fixes:

      1. Volley from about 2 to 2.5 m off the net. Step closer only to finish an easy ball.
      2. Keep the racket up in front of your chest between every shot.
      3. Use most volleys to place the ball deep or at the feet. Save power for high, easy balls.

      Drill: Target volleys. A partner at the back feeds balls to you at the net. Volley each one deep, to a towel or cone placed 1 m off the back glass. Reps: 3 sets of 15, then 15 on the backhand.

      How do I deal with lobs in padel?

      Likely causes:

      • Standing too close to the net.
      • Running backwards while facing the net instead of turning sideways.
      • Waiting too long to decide what to do.

      Three fixes:

      1. Start a step further back at the net (about 2 to 2.5 m away).
      2. Turn sideways at once and move back with side steps or a cross-over step.
      3. Decide early: bandeja if you can reach it, or let it go and play it off the back glass. Both of you go back together.

      Drill: Lob and turn. A partner feeds lobs over you from the back. Turn sideways, move back and catch the ball above your hitting shoulder. Then play a bandeja instead of catching. Reps: 10 catches, then 3 sets of 10 bandejas.

      Why does my bandeja go long?

      Likely causes:

      • Hitting it like a smash, with too much power.
      • Letting the ball drop behind your head.
      • Swinging straight down instead of across and through the ball.

      Three fixes:

      1. Think control, not winner: the bandeja keeps you at the net.
      2. Hit the ball in front of your head, with your body sideways and the non-hitting arm pointing at the ball.
      3. Swing across the ball with a slice and aim to land it near the service line and deep corner, so it does not bounce high off the back glass.

      Drill: Bandeja to the corner. A partner feeds medium lobs. Play bandejas to the opponent's back corner. Count only balls that land past the service line and do not come back high off the glass. Reps: 3 sets of 10.

      Why do my padel smashes come back?

      Likely causes:

      • Smashing balls that are too far back or too low.
      • Hitting into the back glass so the ball bounces back to the opponent.
      • Running back to the net late after the smash.

      Three fixes:

      1. Only smash high balls near the net that you can hit in front of you. Play a bandeja or víbora on the rest.
      2. Aim flat smashes at the side or the feet, not straight into the back glass.
      3. After every overhead, move straight back to your net position.

      Drill: Smash or bandeja call. A partner feeds lobs of different depths. Call 'smash' or 'bandeja' before the ball drops, then play it. Reps: 20 balls, aim for 16 right calls.

      How do I return the serve better in padel?

      Likely causes:

      • Standing too close to the glass or too far forward.
      • A big swing on a ball that is already coming fast.
      • Trying to hit a winner off the return.

      Three fixes:

      1. Wait about 1 m in front of the back glass on your half, ready to move either way.
      2. Use a short swing, like a block, and keep the ball low.
      3. Aim cross-court and low to the server's feet, or lob if the server is already at the net.

      Drill: Return to a target. A partner serves. Return each serve cross-court so it lands before the service line, low over the net. Reps: 3 sets of 10 on each side.

      How can I make my padel serve better?

      Likely causes:

      • Serving high and soft into the middle of the box.
      • Rushing the serve.
      • Staying at the back after serving.

      Three fixes:

      1. Serve low over the net and aim near the side glass or at the receiver's backhand.
      2. Take a breath, bounce the ball, and use a smooth, short swing below the waist.
      3. Move to the net straight after the serve, together with your partner.

      Drill: Serve targets. Put two towels in the service box: one near the side glass, one near the centre line. Serve 10 at each. Reps: 20 serves, count hits.

      Who takes the middle ball in padel?

      Likely causes:

      • No plan for balls down the middle.
      • Not calling the ball.
      • Moving at different times, so a gap opens between you.

      Three fixes:

      1. Agree a middle rule before you play. A common one: the player with the forehand in the middle takes it (the left-side player in a pair of right-handers).
      2. Call 'mine' or 'yours' early and loud on every shared ball.
      3. Move up and back together, about 3 m apart, as if joined by a rope.

      Drill: Middle ball calls. A coach or third player feeds balls down the middle. The player who takes it must call before hitting. A ball with no call counts as a miss. Reps: 3 sets of 10.

      Why am I always late to the ball in padel?

      Likely causes:

      • Flat feet when the opponent hits.
      • Preparing the racket only after the ball bounces.
      • Standing in the wrong place, so every ball is a long run.

      Three fixes:

      1. Do a small hop (a split step) just as the opponent hits, then move.
      2. Turn and take the racket back as soon as you know which side the ball is going.
      3. Use the positions in our positioning guide so you start in the middle of your zone.

      Drill: Split step and shadow. Without a ball: a partner points left or right just after you land a split step. Move two steps, shadow a shot, come back. Reps: 4 rounds of 30 seconds.

      How do I keep my energy in a padel match?

      Likely causes:

      • Long points from always playing from the back.
      • Not warming up, so you start stiff and then fade.
      • Not drinking enough water between games.

      Three fixes:

      1. Win the net more often: it shortens points.
      2. Do a 10-minute warm-up before you play.
      3. Drink at each change of ends and bring enough water for the session.

      Drill: Ten-ball sprints. Partner feeds 10 balls in a row to different corners at a fast pace. Rest 45 seconds. Reps: 4 rounds.

      Why does my elbow hurt after padel?

      Likely causes:

      • Gripping the racket very tightly all the time.
      • A racket that is too heavy, head-heavy or very hard for you.
      • Playing a lot more than usual without rest or a warm-up.

      Three fixes:

      1. See a doctor or physio if pain lasts more than a few days, is sharp, or comes back. Do not play through it.
      2. Hold the racket loosely between shots and squeeze only at contact.
      3. Check your racket: a lighter, low-balance racket with a soft core is usually easier on the arm.

      Drill: Loose grip rally. Only once you are pain free: rally at slow pace and say 'soft' between every shot to remind yourself to relax the grip. Reps: 5 minutes at the start of each session.

      Questions players ask

      What is the most common mistake in padel?

      Playing like tennis: a long, hard, flat swing from the back of the court. In padel the walls give the opponents a second chance, so control and placement win more points than power.

      Why does my padel ball keep going out or hitting the back glass?

      Usually a long swing, too much pace, or a late contact that opens the racket face. Shorten the swing, aim about 1 m over the net at medium pace, and meet the ball in front of you.

      How far from the net should I stand in padel?

      About 2 to 2.5 m when volleying. Closer and lobs go over you; further back and the ball drops at your feet.

      How do I stop lobs going over my head in padel?

      Stand about 2 to 2.5 m from the net, turn sideways as soon as you see the lob, and decide early between a bandeja and letting it come off the back glass. Move back together with your partner.

      Who takes the ball down the middle in padel?

      Agree it before you play. A common rule is that the player with the forehand in the middle takes it, which is the left-side player when both partners are right-handed. Always call 'mine' or 'yours'.

      How do I get better at padel quickly?

      Work on one problem at a time with a simple drill, play regular matches at your level, and take a lesson now and then so a coach can spot the cause of your errors.

      Why does my elbow hurt after playing padel?

      Common reasons are gripping too tightly, a racket that is too heavy, head-heavy or hard, and a sudden increase in how much you play. See a doctor or physio if it lasts, and do not play through pain.
