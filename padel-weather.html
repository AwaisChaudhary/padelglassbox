Can you play padel in the rain? A weather checker for rain, cold, wind and heat.

Tell the checker what the court and sky look like. It says play, play with care, wait or stop, and what to bring: the right balls, layers, a towel and shoes. Then read how rain, cold, wind and heat change the game.

Quick answer

You can play padel in light drizzle on a well-drained outdoor court if the venue allows it and the turf and glass still feel safe, but stop if it rains steadily, if water stands on the court, if anyone slips, or at the first thunder. Wet glass is slippery and the ball skids low off it; soaked balls get heavy and dead. After light rain a modern court is often playable in 15 to 30 minutes: press a towel into the turf, and if it comes up wet, wait. In cold weather balls lose bounce, so warm up for about 15 minutes and dress in layers.

Bring and do

    Can you play padel in the rain?

    It depends on how much rain, and on the court. Indoor courts are not affected.

    Light drizzle

    Often playable on a well-drained outdoor court, if the venue allows it and the turf, glass and your grip still feel safe. Expect a slower, lower game.

    Steady rain

    Water collects on the glass and the turf. The ball skids low off the walls and you lose grip on turns. Most players stop or move indoors.

    Puddles or standing water

    Stop. Standing water is where slips happen, and the bounce becomes random.

    Snow or ice

    Do not play. On snow the ball no longer bounces properly, and ice on turf is dangerous.

    Thunder or lightning

    Stop at once and go inside a building or a closed car. Wait 30 minutes after the last thunder. An open-sided padel roof is not shelter.

    Why rain matters: a padel court is artificial grass with sand brushed in, and the sand is what lets you slide and stop. Water makes that grip unpredictable, and wet glass is slippery for both feet and ball. A wet ball can also slip on the racket face. Many clubs close outdoor courts in heavy rain, so check the booking app's cancellation rules: see padel booking apps.

    How long after rain can you play padel?

    Modern court: 15 to 30 minutes

    A newer court with good drainage is often playable 15 to 30 minutes after light rain stops.

    Older court: hours

    Older turf or poor drainage can hold water for hours. Look for dark, shiny patches near the service lines and in the corners.

    The towel test

    Press a dry towel into the turf near the service line. If it comes up wet, the court is not ready yet.

    Dry the glass

    Wipe the glass at ball height with a towel. Wet glass makes the ball slide down instead of bouncing out.

    Playing padel in winter and cold weather

    Balls go dead

    In the cold, padel balls lose bounce and feel slower. Smashes stay in the court and volleys die near the back glass. Keep balls in your bag, not in a cold car, and open a new tube if they feel flat.

    Dress in layers

    It is easier to take a layer off than to add one. Bring a spare dry top in case of a shower.

    Warm up for longer

    Plan about 15 minutes to warm up and 15 to cool down, so a one-hour booking needs about 90 minutes of your time. Cold and wet raise the injury risk.

    Protect your arm

    Dead, damp balls make your arm work harder. Light band work for the wrist, elbow and shoulder before you play helps. See padel elbow.

    How do you play padel in the wind?

    Keep lobs lower

    Above the glass the wind moves the ball much more. A high lob can drift half a court. Play lower, flatter lobs, or keep the ball under the glass height.

    Watch the direction

    Playing into the wind, you can lob harder. With the wind behind you, lobs fly long, so take pace off.

    Let more balls bounce

    Overheads are hard to time in gusts. Letting a ball come off the glass is often safer than a smash.

    Stay patient

    The other pair hate the wind too. Fewer risky shots usually wins windy points.

    Playing padel in hot weather

    Faster, bouncier game

    Warm air makes balls and glass livelier. The ball comes off the walls faster and lobs travel further.

    Book early or late

    Midday sun on glass courts gets very hot. Early morning or evening slots are cooler.

    Drink and rest

    Bring more water than you think, use the change of ends to drink in the shade, and stop if you feel dizzy or sick. General advice, not medical advice.

    Use the sun

    If the sun is in an opponent's eyes, a lob is a good option. Courts often run end to end, so the sun can move along the court during a session.

    When balls are finished in any weather: when to change padel balls. Wet shoes: dry them at room temperature, never on a radiator or in a dryer, and check the sole: one that is fine on dry turf can slide on wet turf. See padel shoes. Before a cold session, use the warm-up timer.

    Kit for cold and wet days

    A fresh tube of balls, shoes with good tread and a few overgrips (they get slippery when damp) make the biggest difference outdoors in winter.

    Questions players ask

    Can you play padel in the rain?

    In light drizzle on a well-drained outdoor court, often yes, if the venue allows it and the court still feels safe. Stop in steady rain, if water stands on the court, if anyone slips, or at the first thunder. Indoor courts are not affected.

    Is padel cancelled if it rains?

    It depends on the club. Many close outdoor courts in heavy rain or move you indoors. Check the club's or booking app's weather and cancellation rules before you go.

    How long after rain can you play padel?

    A modern, well-drained court is often playable 15 to 30 minutes after light rain stops; an older court can take hours. Press a towel into the turf: if it comes up wet, wait.

    Can you play padel in winter?

    Yes. Dress in layers, warm up for about 15 minutes, use a fresh tube of balls because cold balls lose bounce, and check the court is dry. Stop if it rains or snows or the surface gets slippery.

    Why do padel balls feel dead in the cold?

    Padel balls are pressurised. In the cold the air inside has less push, so the ball bounces less and feels slower. In heat it is the opposite: they fly faster.

    Can you play padel in the wind?

    Yes, but lobs and overheads get hard above the glass. Keep the ball lower, take pace off lobs with the wind behind you and let more balls come off the glass.
