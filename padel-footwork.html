Padel footwork and the split step, with a reaction trainer.

Good padel footwork is mostly small things: a split step as they hit, short steps to the ball and a quick recovery. Learn the six movements, check the common mistakes, then use the trainer: it cues your split step and flashes a direction to move, like a coach with a ball basket.

Quick answer

The split step is a small hop onto both feet that lands just as your opponent hits the ball, so you are balanced and ready to move any way. After it, use short side shuffles for close balls, a crossover step for wide ones, turn sideways to go back for lobs, take small steps before you hit, and recover to the middle of your half after every shot.

The six footwork movements in padel

Move 1

Split step

A small hop onto both feet, landing just as an opponent hits the ball. You land balanced on the balls of your feet, feet a little wider than your shoulders, ready to push off in any direction.

Move 2

Side shuffle

Short sideways steps without crossing your feet, for balls a step or two away. Your shoulders stay facing the net, so you are ready to volley.

Move 3

Crossover step

For a ball further away, turn your hips and cross one leg over the other to cover ground fast, then go back to small steps before you hit.

Move 4

Turn and run back for a lob

Turn sideways and move back with side or crossover steps, never backwards on your heels. If the lob is good, keep turning and let it come off the back glass.

Move 5

Small adjustment steps

The last two or three steps before a shot are small. They set your distance to the ball so you can hit it in front of your body.

Move 6

Recover

After every shot, move back towards your best position at once: middle of your half, and level with your partner. Hit, then recover, every time.

When do you split step in padel?

Every time an opponent is about to hit, wherever you are.

Timing

Start the hop as they swing, so you land at the moment their racket hits the ball. Land too early and you are flat-footed again; too late and you are in the air when you need to move.

Size

Small. A few centimetres off the ground is enough. At the net it is often just a quick bounce on your toes, because the ball arrives fast.

At the net

Racket up in front, split as they hit, then one or two steps to the volley. See how to volley.

At the back

Split, read whether the ball will hit the back glass, and move early to leave space between you and the wall. See playing off the back glass.

On the return

Split as the server hits, then step in to the ball. See how to return serve.

With your partner

Move as a pair: forward together, back together. When one goes to the net, the other follows. See where to stand.

Common padel footwork mistakes

No split step

You are still moving, or flat-footed, when they hit. You start late and arrive late.

Watching your own shot

Admiring the ball instead of recovering leaves a gap for the next one.

Rushing to the net

Running in behind a weak ball gets you lobbed. Move forward when your shot gives you time.

Hitting off balance

Arrive early, set your feet, then hit. A late, stretched shot usually goes up or into the net.

Recovering too wide

Drifting to the side wall opens the middle. Recover to the middle of your half, not the edge.

Crossing feet at the net

Crossing your legs while volleying makes you slow to change direction. Shuffle instead.

Questions players ask

What is a split step in padel?

A small hop onto both feet that lands just as your opponent hits the ball. You land balanced on the balls of your feet, so you can push off quickly in any direction.

When should you split step in padel?

Every time an opponent is about to hit: start the hop as they swing and land as the racket meets the ball. At the net it can be just a quick bounce on your toes.

How do you improve footwork in padel?

Add a split step before every opponent's shot, use small steps to get to the ball, recover to the middle of your half after every shot, and practise with shadow drills and a feeder a few minutes each session.

Should you cross your feet in padel?

Not for short movements: shuffle sideways instead, especially at the net. A crossover step is useful to cover a big distance, such as going back for a lob, before small steps into the shot.

How do you move back for a lob in padel?

Turn sideways first, then move back with side or crossover steps. Don't backpedal on your heels. If the lob is too good, keep going and play it off the back glass.

Is footwork different in padel and tennis?

Yes. The padel court is smaller and the walls give second chances, so steps are shorter, you split step more often, and you need to leave space for the ball off the glass.
