How to hit a padel forehand (and fix the one you have)

The padel forehand is shorter and calmer than a tennis forehand. Learn the four steps, when to hit off the bounce or wait for the glass, and use the fault fixer to find out why your forehand goes into the net, flies long or comes late.

Quick answer

To hit a padel forehand, take the racket back early with a short backswing, turn sideways so your weight can move from the back foot to the front foot, and meet the ball out in front of your body at about hip height. Push through the back of the ball with the face pointing at your target, and keep the finish short. If the ball bounces before the service line, play it off the bounce; if it bounces after the line, let it come off the back glass.

    How do you hit a forehand in padel, step by step?

    Step 1

    Prepare early, swing short

    Take the racket back as soon as you see the ball coming to your forehand. Padel needs little power, so a short backswing is enough, and it gives you more time to read the ball.

    Step 2

    Turn sideways

    Turn your shoulders and stand side-on, so your weight can move from the back foot to the front foot. An open stance turns the shot into an arm-only hit.

    Step 3

    Contact in front, at hip height

    Let the ball come to the side of your body and meet it out in front, at about hip height. Move your feet so you are not too close to the ball or reaching for it.

    Step 4

    Push through, finish short

    Push through the back of the ball on a flat, front-to-back path, with the face pointing at your target. No big tennis follow-through over the shoulder.

    Grip: a continental grip works for every padel shot; some coaches let improving players turn the hand one bevel towards the forehand for drives from the back only. See how to hold a padel racket.

    Off the bounce or off the glass?

    The service line is your decision line.

    The ball bouncesPlay itWhy
    Before the service lineStraight off the bounceIt will not reach the back glass with enough pace to play after it
    After the service lineLet it hit the back glass, then play itThe glass slows it down and gives you more time and a better contact point
    Slow ball, any depthUsually off the bounceYou have time to set up
    Fast ball, deepOff the glassLet the wall take the pace

    Drill, "yellow card, red card" (LTA Padel): play normal points. If a ball bounces past the service line and you hit it before the glass, that is a yellow card. A second one in the same rally is a red card and you lose the point. More on the glass: playing off the back glass.

    Padel forehand vs tennis forehand

    Tennis forehandPadel forehand
    BackswingBig loopShort, racket back early
    Contact heightOften around the waist to chest, with topspinAbout hip height, flatter or with a little slice
    FinishOver the shoulderShort, towards the target
    Main aimPace and depth to win the pointControl and placement: keep it low and away from the net pair
    WristOften brushes up for topspinFirmer and quieter

    What are the most common padel forehand mistakes?

    Mistake 1

    Hitting too hard

    The forehand feels like your strongest shot, so you swing harder. Most forehand errors are pace errors: aim for control and vary the pace.

    Mistake 2

    Too much slice

    Chopping down on the ball makes it float or die in the net. Push through the ball so slices stay low off the glass.

    Mistake 3

    Late preparation

    If the racket goes back only when the ball arrives, you rush the finish. Get it back as soon as you know it is a forehand.

    Mistake 4

    Standing still

    Not turning and not moving the feet leaves you too close or too far. Take small steps until the ball is at the side of your body.

    What about the backhand?

    The padel backhand works on the same ideas: racket back early, short backswing, turn sideways, contact in front, push through towards the target. Most players hit it one-handed with the continental grip, which also makes it easier to play off the glass and at the net. If a ball is high on your backhand side, it is often a chance to play a bandeja or a volley instead.

    Questions players ask

    How do you hit a forehand in padel?

    Take the racket back early with a short backswing, turn sideways, meet the ball in front of you at about hip height and push through it towards your target with a short finish.

    What grip do you use for a padel forehand?

    The continental grip works for every padel shot. Some coaches let improving players turn the hand slightly towards an eastern forehand grip for drives from the back, but never at the net.

    Why does my padel forehand go into the net?

    Usually you chop down with too much slice, let the ball drop too low, or stand too close. Push through the back of the ball and meet it in front at about hip height.

    Should I hit the forehand before or after the glass?

    If the ball bounces before the service line, play it off the bounce. If it bounces after the service line, let it hit the back glass first.

    Is a padel forehand like a tennis forehand?

    No. It uses a shorter backswing, a flatter path, a short finish and much less pace. Placement and keeping the ball low matter more than power.

    How hard should I hit a padel forehand?

    Less hard than you think. Most forehand errors come from overhitting. Control the pace and aim low, cross-court or at the feet of the net player.
