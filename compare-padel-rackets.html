Quick answer

When you compare two padel rackets, look at the specs in this order: shape (where the sweet spot sits), balance (head-heavy or head-light), core hardness, face material and, last, weight. A 5 to 10 g gap in weight matters less than a change of shape or balance. Round, head-light, soft rackets suit beginners and sore arms; diamond, head-heavy, hard rackets suit advanced attacking players.

Which specs matter most when you compare padel rackets?

From biggest to smallest effect on how a racket plays.

Spec 1

Shape

Decides where the sweet spot sits. Round: low and wide, the most forgiving. Teardrop: a little higher, a mix of control and power. Diamond: high and small, the most power when you hit it right.

Spec 2

Balance

How the weight is spread. Above about 265 mm feels head-heavy: more weight behind smashes, slower at the net. Below about 255 mm feels quick and easy. Compare balance numbers from the same brand or shop, because they measure differently.

Spec 3

Core

Soft foam holds the ball longer: easy power at slow speeds and kinder on the arm. Hard foam gives the ball back faster, but only if you swing fast. Medium suits most club players.

Spec 4

Face

Fibreglass is softer and more forgiving. Carbon is stiffer and more precise, and passes on more vibration. Higher carbon numbers (12K, 18K) are about weave and feel, not automatic quality.

Spec 5

Weight

Matters, but least of the five. Rackets from the same model can differ by a few grams, so don't choose on a 5 g gap. A 15 to 20 g gap you will feel.

Spec 6

Thickness and surface

Almost all adult rackets are 38 mm thick. A rough or sandy surface adds a little grip for spin. Neither changes much compared with the five above.

Questions players ask

How do I compare two padel rackets?

Compare the specs in order of how much they change the feel: shape first, then balance, core hardness, face material and weight. Use the numbers from the same shop or brand, and try both if you can.

What matters more in a padel racket: weight or balance?

Balance usually changes the feel more than a small weight difference. A racket 10 mm more head-heavy feels heavier to swing than one 10 g heavier with the same balance.

Is a 5 g difference in padel rackets noticeable?

Hardly. Rackets of the same model can vary by a few grams. Most players start to feel a difference at about 15 to 20 g.

Is a diamond or round padel racket better?

Neither is better in general. Round is more forgiving and easier to control; diamond gives more power on smashes if you hit the high sweet spot. Beginners and players with sore arms usually do better with round.

Is a more expensive padel racket better?

Not automatically. Price often follows carbon type and the brand's top model. A racket whose shape, balance and core fit your game will feel better than an expensive one that doesn't.

Can I compare balance numbers from different brands?

Be careful. Brands and shops measure balance in slightly different ways, so compare numbers from the same source, or measure both rackets yourself the same way.
