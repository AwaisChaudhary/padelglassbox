How to serve in padel: 6 steps and a serve practice tracker.

Learn a reliable padel serve step by step, choose where to aim, then log a practice basket to see your serve percentage and what to fix.

Quick answer

To serve in padel: stand sideways behind the service line with a continental grip, drop the ball just in front of your front foot, let it bounce once, and hit it at or below waist height with a short low-to-high swing. Aim deep into the diagonal service box: wide to the side glass, at the receiver's body, or down the T. Then move up to the net. You get two attempts, and placement matters more than power.

How do you serve in padel, step by step?

1. Stand sideways

Stand behind the service line, on the right half for the first point of each game. Turn your front shoulder toward the box you are serving into. Feet about shoulder-width apart, knees soft.

2. Use a continental grip

Hold the racket like a hammer, the same grip as for volleys. See how to hold a padel racket.

3. Drop, don't toss

Drop the ball beside your body, just in front of your front foot. Let it bounce once. Do not throw it up: the ball must bounce before you hit it.

4. Hit at or below the waist

Wait for the ball to start falling, around knee to thigh height. Contact must be at or below your waist, with at least one foot on the ground.

5. Short swing, low to high

A compact swing that moves from low to high. Power comes from moving your weight from the back foot to the front foot, not from a big arm swing.

6. Move to the net

Follow the serve forward to your volley spot, about 2 to 2.5 m from the net. The serve is your way to take the net, where most points are won.

Where should you aim your padel serve?

Aim deep, into the back third of the box. Then pick one of three targets.

TargetWhy
Wide, into the side glassThe ball bounces off the side glass and the receiver has to turn. Works at every level.
At the body (hip)The receiver has no room to swing and often plays a weak, high return.
Down the TLands near the centre line, often on the receiver's backhand. Narrows their angles.
Mix themChange target and speed so the receiver cannot settle.

Padel serve drills

10 per target

Serve 10 balls at each of the three targets and log them above. Repeat on the left side. Watch your "hit the target" number grow week by week.

First-serve percentage

Serve 20 first serves at match pace. Aim to get at least 8 in 10 in. If you are under that, slow down before you add spin.

Serve and get to the net

Put a marker about one racket length from the centre line, level with the second post of the side fence. Serve, then reach the marker before the return. Five in a row wins.

Questions players ask

How do you serve in padel?

Stand behind the service line, drop the ball and let it bounce once, then hit it at or below waist height into the service box diagonally opposite. Use a short, low-to-high swing and move to the net after the serve.

Is the padel serve underarm?

Yes. You must hit the ball at or below waist height, so the serve is underarm or side-arm. Overhead and jump serves are not allowed.

Where should I aim my padel serve?

Deep in the box, at one of three targets: wide into the side glass, at the receiver's body, or down the T near the centre line. Mix targets and speed.

How many serves do you get in padel?

Two. If the first is a fault, you serve again. Two faults in a row lose the point. A serve that clips the net and lands in the box is a let and is replayed.

Should I serve hard in padel?

Usually not. Very few padel serves are unreturnable, so placement and getting to the net matter more than speed. Many players serve at about two thirds pace with slice.

What is a good first-serve percentage in padel?

Aim to get at least 8 in 10 first serves in. Missing first serves gives away free pressure on your second serve.
