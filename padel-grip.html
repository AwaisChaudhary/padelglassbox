How to hold a padel racket.

One grip does almost everything in padel. Find it with the steps below, tap a shot to see which grip it uses, and run the grip check to fix what is making your hand tired or the ball fly.

Quick answer

Use the continental grip for almost every padel shot. Hold the racket with the frame edge pointing up and down and shake hands with the handle, as if holding a hammer: the V between your thumb and first finger sits on the top edge. Keep your hand relaxed between shots, wear the wrist strap, and use the same grip for forehands, backhands, volleys and overheads.

Continental grip

How do you find the continental grip?

Step 1

Stand the racket on its edge

Hold the racket by the throat with your free hand, so the frame points straight up and down and the faces point left and right.

Step 2

Shake hands with it

Put your playing hand on the handle as if you were shaking someone's hand, or holding a hammer to knock in a nail.

Step 3

Check the V

Look down at your hand. The V shape between your thumb and first finger should sit on the top edge of the handle (bevel 1 in the drawing).

Step 4

Leave a small gap

Wrap your fingers round. Spread them a little and keep a small gap between your palm and the handle. Don't press your palm flat on it.

Step 5

Strap on first

Put the wrist strap on before you take your grip. Every padel racket has one, and many clubs ask you to wear it.

Step 6

Reset between shots

After each shot, let your free hand touch the throat of the racket and turn the grip back to continental if it has moved.

Coaches agree the continental grip is the base. The eastern options are extras for improving players and are never used at the net. If you are in your first months of padel, keep one grip for everything.

How tight should you hold a padel racket?

Relaxed between shots

Hold the racket loosely while you wait and move. A loose hand reacts faster at the net.

A little firmer at contact

Squeeze a bit more just as you hit, then relax again straight after. Don't hold at full strength all the time.

Why it matters

Gripping hard all the time locks your wrist, tires the forearm and is linked to elbow pain. See padel injuries for more on padel elbow.

Is my padel grip too small or too big?

The finger test

Take the continental grip. There should be a small gap between your fingertips and the base of your thumb, about the width of one finger of your other hand. No gap means the handle is thin; a big gap means it is thick.

Too small

If the handle is too thin, you tend to squeeze harder as the match goes on. Add an overgrip, or wrap with more overlap.

Too big

A handle that is too thick is hard to turn and hold relaxed. Wrap the overgrip with less overlap, or remove an extra layer.

Overgrips change the size

Many padel rackets come in one handle size, so players change the feel with overgrips. Each extra layer, or more overlap as you wrap, makes the handle a little thicker.

How do you put on an overgrip?

  1. Pull off the old overgrip and its finishing tape.
  2. Put the wrist strap on its holder so it doesn't get in the way.
  3. Start at the bottom of the handle, by the butt cap. Peel the backing and stick the thin end there.
  4. Turn the racket slowly and wrap up the handle, keeping each turn parallel and overlapping the last one by the same amount.
  5. Stop at the top of the handle. Cut the end straight across and fix it with the finishing tape.

How often to change it: brand guides range from every two or three sessions for club players to at least every two months. Change it sooner if it looks dirty, changes colour or stops feeling sticky. More on this in racket care.

Questions players ask

What grip do you use in padel?

The continental grip. It is the base grip for almost every shot: serve, return, volleys on both sides, bandeja, víbora, smash and balls off the glass.

How do you find the continental grip in padel?

Stand the racket on its edge and shake hands with the handle, like holding a hammer. The V between your thumb and first finger sits on the top edge of the handle.

Do you change grip in padel?

Mostly no. Beginners should use the continental grip for everything. Some coaches let improving players turn the hand one bevel for some forehands or high backhands from the back of the court, but never at the net.

How tight should I hold my padel racket?

Loosely between shots and a little firmer just as you hit. Squeezing hard all the time tires your forearm and is linked to elbow pain.

How do I know if my padel grip is too small?

Take the continental grip. If your fingertips dig into your palm with no gap, or you find yourself squeezing harder as you play, the handle is probably too thin. Add an overgrip or overlap it more.

Is the padel grip the same as in tennis?

The continental grip is the same one tennis players use to serve and volley. Many tennis players use a different grip for forehands, but in padel the continental is used for nearly everything.

Is the grip different for left-handed players?

It is the same continental grip, just mirrored. If a coach tells you to turn one bevel for a forehand, a left-hander turns the other way.
