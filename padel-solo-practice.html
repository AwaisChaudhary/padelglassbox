Solo padel practice: a drill plan and timer for when you have no partner.

No partner tonight? Pick where you can practise, what you want to work on and how long you have. You get a step-by-step session with a timer that beeps at every change, so you can just follow it. Works on an empty padel court, against any solid wall, or in a small space at home.

Quick answer

Yes, you can practise padel alone. The most useful solo drills are wall volleys (stand 2 to 3 m from a wall and volley without a bounce), wall groundstrokes from 5 to 6 m, serves to targets, self-feeds off the back glass on an empty court, ball-control bounce-ups and shadow swings with a split step. Twenty minutes, two or three times a week, is enough to feel the difference.

    Can you practise padel on your own?

    Yes. You can't play points alone, but most of what makes a padel player better can be trained solo: clean contact, soft hands at the net, a repeatable serve, early footwork and reading the glass. Coaches use the same building blocks in lessons. What you can't train alone is reading opponents and working with a partner, so mix solo sessions with real games and use them to fix one thing at a time.

    What are the best padel wall drills?

    Drill 1

    Forehand wall volleys

    Stand 2 to 3 m from the wall and keep the ball going without a bounce. Short punch, racket head up. Start with 10 in a row, then 25.

    Drill 2

    Backhand and alternate volleys

    The backhand volley is where most club players lose points. Do a full minute backhand only, then alternate sides.

    Drill 3

    Wall groundstrokes

    From 5 to 6 m, one bounce, controlled swing to waist height on the wall. A smooth rally of 20 beats ten hard hits.

    Drill 4

    Serve to a tape line

    A line at about 90 cm copies the net. Land the ball just above it with a below-the-waist serve, then aim left and right of a mark.

    Use a wall you are allowed to hit, away from windows and cars. Padel balls are slightly less bouncy than tennis balls, so stand a little closer than you would for tennis.

    How do you practise padel serves alone?

    Book or use a quiet court, put two targets in each service box (the corner by the side glass and the middle line, the T), and hit 10 serves at each from both sides. Remember the rules: bounce the ball behind the service line, hit it at or below waist height, and keep at least one foot on the ground. Count how many land in the box and how many reach the target, and write it down so you can see progress. More on the technique in how to serve in padel, and check what is legal with the serve checker.

    How long and how often should you practise alone?

    Your timeA good plan
    10 minutesWarm up, 2 or 3 drills on one weakness, done. Great before a match or on a busy day.
    20 minutesWarm up, 5 or 6 drills with one main focus plus ball control, short cool-down.
    30 minutesWarm up, 8 to 10 drills, focus first while you are fresh, then mixed drills, cool-down.

    Two or three short solo sessions a week usually help more than one long one, because you repeat the movement more often. Keep the quality high: when your contact gets sloppy, stop the drill and move on. If you want a full week plan with games, lessons and solo time, build one with the training plan generator.

    What mistakes do people make when practising alone?

    Mistake 1

    Hitting too hard

    Speed hides bad contact. Hit at 60 to 70% and keep the ball going; power comes later.

    Mistake 2

    Standing still

    Real padel is moving and recovering. Add a split step before every ball, even in wall drills.

    Mistake 3

    Only the favourite shot

    Most people drill the forehand they already like. Spend at least half the time on the weaker side.

    Mistake 4

    No targets

    Without a target you can't tell if you are improving. Use cans, towels or tape and count your hits.

    Questions players ask

    Can you practise padel alone?

    Yes. Wall volleys, wall groundstrokes, serves to targets, self-feeds off the glass on an empty court, ball-control bounce-ups and shadow swings can all be done alone.

    What is the best padel drill to do alone?

    Wall volleys: stand 2 to 3 m from a solid wall and volley without letting the ball bounce, forehand, then backhand, then alternating. It trains contact, soft hands and quick reactions.

    How do I practise my padel serve alone?

    Put targets in the corner and the middle of each service box and hit 10 legal serves at each target from both sides, counting how many land. At a wall, aim just above a tape line at about 90 cm.

    Can I practise padel against a wall at home?

    Yes, if the wall is solid and safe to hit, away from windows. With no wall you can still do bounce-ups, catch control, shadow swings and footwork in about 2 m of space.

    How long should a solo padel session be?

    Ten to thirty minutes is enough. Two or three short sessions a week usually help more than one long one.

    Can I book a padel court to practise on my own?

    Many clubs allow it, often at quiet times. Ask the club first, as some only allow bookings for two or four players.
