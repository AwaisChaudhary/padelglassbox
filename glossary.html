Padel words, decoded.

Padel borrows lots of Spanish. Search any word you've heard on court, see how to say it, and what it means in English.

Quick answer

The padel words you'll hear most are bandeja (a controlled overhead, said ban-DEH-ha), víbora (a spin smash, VEE-bo-ra), chiquita (a soft low ball, chee-KEE-ta), globo (a lob), pared (wall) and malla (the metal fence, MAH-ya). This glossary has 30 terms, 20 of them Spanish with a simple way to say each one.

How to read the sounds: the part in capitals is stressed. In Spanish a "v" sounds close to a soft "b", "j" and "g" before e or i sound like the "h" in "hat", and "ll" sounds like the "y" in "yes" in most of Spain. Accents vary between countries, so you will hear small differences on court.

Which Spanish padel words should beginners learn first?

Calls on court

Mía (MEE-a, "mine") and tuya (TOO-ya, "yours") tell your partner who takes the ball. Fuera (FWEH-ra) means out. Short, clear calls stop both of you going for the same ball.

The shots you'll hear

Bandeja, víbora, globo, chiquita and bajada. See each one move in the shot library.

The court

Pared and cristal for the glass, malla for the fence, and red (RED) for the net. The wall rules checker shows which ones you can use.

Questions players ask

How do you pronounce bandeja in padel?

Say ban-DEH-ha. The Spanish j sounds like the h in hat. Bandeja means tray, because you hold the racket face up like a waiter's tray.

How do you pronounce víbora?

Say VEE-bo-ra, with the stress on the first part. In Spanish the v sounds close to a soft b, so you will also hear BEE-bo-ra.

What does mía and tuya mean in padel?

Mía means mine and tuya means yours. Partners shout them to say who will take a ball down the middle.

What is the difference between a bandeja and a víbora?

Both are overheads played from near the net. A bandeja is flatter and controlled to keep your place at the net; a víbora has more sidespin and speed and is more attacking.

What does malla mean in padel?

Malla is the metal mesh fence around the sides of the court. Balls that hit it bounce unpredictably, and you can't play off your own fence.

What are drive and revés in padel?

Drive is the player on the right side of the court and revés is the player on the left. In Spanish the words also mean forehand and backhand.
