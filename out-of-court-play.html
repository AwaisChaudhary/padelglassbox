Padel out-of-court play: when can you leave the court?

The ball bounced and flew out through the door. Can you chase it? Choose the type of court, tap what happened, and get the call with the FIP rule number.

Quick answer

Only on courts built for it. If the ball bounces in your court and then goes out over the side wall or through the door, you may run out and hit it back before it bounces a second time, if the court has door openings and a clear area outside (at least 3 m wide and 4 m long). On courts without that space, a ball that bounces and leaves the court wins the point for the team that hit it.

What is out-of-court play in padel?

The idea

Your opponents hit a good shot: it bounces in your court and flies out over the side wall or through the door. On some courts you may follow it out and hit it back from outside. In Spanish this is called salida de pista (sa-LEE-da de PEES-ta, "leaving the court").

Who allows it

FIP rule 16 allows it whenever the court meets the safety conditions. In practice the club or organiser tells you if it's on. Many club courts don't have the space, so ask before the match.

The one rule to remember

You must hit the ball before it bounces a second time, and before it touches anything outside the court. Your return then follows the normal rules: over the net and onto their ground first.

How do you play a ball outside the court?

1. Read it early

A hard smash or a fast ball off the side glass near the net is the one that goes out. When you see it coming, start moving to the door.

2. Go through the nearest door

Use the opening on the side where the ball is going. Call to your partner so they know you are going out.

3. Hit it before the second bounce

Let it drop, then hit it before it lands again outside. A high lob back over the wall gives you time to run back in.

4. Partner covers the court

While you are out, your partner holds the middle. Get back in as fast as you can: the other team will aim for the empty side.

Stay safe: only run out where the area is clear, and watch for people walking by. If the space isn't there, let the ball go.

Questions players ask

Can you play outside the court in padel?

Yes, but only on courts built for it, with door openings and a clear area outside, and when the club allows it. If the ball bounces in your court and goes out, you may run out and hit it back before its second bounce.

What happens if the ball goes out of the court in padel?

If it bounced in your court first, it was a good shot by the other team. Where out-of-court play is allowed you may chase it. Where it isn't, the other team wins the point.

How much space do you need for out-of-court play?

The FIP rules ask for two openings per side and a clear area outside of at least 3 m wide (4 m recommended), 4 m long and 3 m high, with nothing standing in it.

Does the return have to come back over the wall?

The FIP rules don't say so. A return from outside counts if it crosses the net and bounces in the other team's court before touching their walls or fence. It can come back over the wall or in through the door.

Can the other team volley a ball hit from outside the court?

Yes. There is no special rule for these returns, so the other team may volley it like any other ball.

What is salida de pista in padel?

Salida de pista is Spanish for leaving the court. It means running out of the court through the door to play a ball that has bounced and gone out, on courts where this is allowed.

Is it a let if a ball from another court rolls onto ours?

Yes. If an object such as another ball comes onto your court during the point, it's a let and you replay the point. Call it straight away, or you lose the right to the let.
