30-day padel beginner plan: a day-by-day checklist.

New to padel? Follow one short task a day for 30 days: 10 court sessions, short home tasks and rest days. Tick each day off and the plan remembers where you are.

Quick answer

A good first month of padel is about 10 court sessions (two or three a week) with short tasks between them. Week 1: rules, the grip and balls off the back glass. Week 2: the serve, where to stand and a first scored game. Week 3: volleys, the lob and tracking your errors. Week 4: fix your biggest weakness and play new people. Rest at least one day between hard sessions.

    Book a court or a beginner clinic. Hire a racket. Rally gently from the back and let balls come off the back glass.

    Read which balls you can play off the glass and the fence, and which are out.

    Spend 15 minutes only on balls off the back glass. Wait, turn sideways, hit softly to the middle.

    Watch one pro match. Notice how both players move up and back together.

  1. Day 8: Serve at home At home, 10-20 min

    Practise the serve motion: bounce, hit below the waist, point at the far box. 20 times.

What should I learn first in padel?

WeekMain skillYou know it's working when
1Grip and the back glassYou can let a ball come off the back glass and return it softly most of the time
2Serve and positionMost of your serves land in, and you move up and back with your partner
3Volley and lobYou can keep a short volley rally going and push opponents back with a lob
4Fixing one weaknessYour errors on your worst shot go down from one tracked match to the next

How often should a beginner play padel?

Two or three times a week

Enough to remember what you learned last time, with time to recover. Once a week still works; the plan just takes longer.

Rest between hard sessions

New players often get sore calves, shoulders or elbows. Warm up first and stop if something hurts. See padel injuries and prevention.

Take a lesson early

One beginner clinic in week 1 makes the walls and the serve much easier. See what lessons cost.

Questions players ask

How long does it take to learn padel?

Most people can rally in their first session. In about a month of playing two or three times a week you can usually serve, play off the back glass and play a full scored game.

What should I practise first in padel?

The continental grip, letting the ball come off the back glass before you hit it, and a steady underarm serve. Power comes later.

How many times a week should a beginner play padel?

Two or three sessions a week with a rest day between is a good start. Once a week also works, just more slowly.

Do I need lessons to start padel?

No, but one beginner clinic early on makes the walls and the serve much easier to learn.

Can I learn padel in 30 days?

You can learn the basics and play proper games in 30 days. Getting good takes longer: steady club level usually takes a year or two.
