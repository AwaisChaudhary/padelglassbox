Padel partner communication: what to say and who takes the middle ball.

Most doubles mix-ups happen down the middle, between the two of you. Use the decider to settle whose ball it is in each situation, learn the eight calls good pairs use, and agree a few rules before the match.

Quick answer

In padel, call the ball early: "mine" or "yours" before it crosses the net. If both players are right-handed, the middle usually belongs to the left-side player, because it is their forehand, including lobs down the middle. With a left-hander on the right side, both forehands meet in the middle, so agree one rule before you play, such as the player further forward or the one who called first.

Padel calls: what to say to your partner

Short words, said early. Many clubs also use the Spanish ones.

"Mine" / "Yo"

I'm taking it. Say it early and loud, before the ball crosses the net, not as you hit it.

"Yours" / "Tuya"

You take it. Just as important as "mine": it stops both of you leaving the ball.

"Switch" / "Cambio"

A lob went over one of you and the other is running to get it. Swap sides and keep going.

"Glass" / "Pared"

Let it bounce and come off the wall: you will have more time after the glass.

"Out" / "Leave it"

The ball will hit the back or side glass or fence directly without bouncing. Don't touch it: it's your point.

"Up" / "Back"

Move forward to the net together, or go back together. Pairs that move as one leave fewer gaps.

"Middle"

Before the point: the next ball goes down the middle. Two players reaching for it often clash or both leave it.

"Next one" / "Good try"

After a mistake, a short positive word. Give information, not emotion.

Why "out" matters: a ball that hits your back or side glass or fence before it bounces is out, so letting it go wins the point. Check the exact rule in wall and fence rules.

What to agree before the match

Who covers the middle

Forehand takes it is the usual default. If you are a left and right-hander pair, decide whose it is when both forehands meet: see the decider above.

Who takes overheads

Lobs down the middle usually go to the player with the more reliable overhead, often the left-side player. See bandeja vs víbora.

What happens when you are lobbed

Agree that when one of you is lobbed, the partner runs and calls "switch" if needed, and you both go back together.

Default plays

For example: short ball, attack the middle; under pressure, lob. Simple patterns stop you both guessing.

Serve signals

Some pairs use a hand signal behind the back: where the serve goes, and whether the net player will cross. Keep it simple.

Questions players ask

Who takes the middle ball in padel?

If both players are right-handed, usually the left-side player, because it is their forehand. With a left-hander on the right, both forehands meet in the middle, so agree a rule first, such as the player further forward or the player who called first.

Who takes the lob down the middle in padel?

Usually the left-side player for two right-handers, because they can play it on the forehand side with a bandeja or smash. In other pairs, the player with the more reliable overhead.

What do padel players say to each other?

Short calls: "mine" and "yours" to claim a ball, "switch" when you swap sides after a lob, "glass" to let the ball come off the wall, "out" for a ball that will hit the glass or fence without bouncing, and "up" or "back" to move together.

When should you call the ball in padel?

Early, before the ball crosses the net. A late call makes both players hesitate, which is how middle balls get missed or rackets clash.

What does "cambio" mean in padel?

It is Spanish for "change" or "switch". You call it when a lob goes over your partner and you run across to take it, so you both swap sides.

How do you talk to your padel partner after a mistake?

Keep it short and positive, and give information rather than emotion, such as "good try, next time lob". Save bigger talks for the change of ends.
