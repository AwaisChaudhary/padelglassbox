Padel racket materials: carbon, fibreglass, EVA and foam explained

Racket labels are full of words like 3K carbon, hard EVA and 3D rough face. Put in what the label says and see what it means for comfort, power and spin. Compare two rackets side by side.

Quick answer

A padel racket has a foam core (EVA rubber or softer foam) inside two face skins (fibreglass, carbon or a mix), with a hollow frame. Fibreglass faces and soft cores feel softer and more forgiving and suit most beginners. Carbon faces and hard EVA cores feel stiffer and give more power on fast swings but forgive less. Numbers like 3K, 12K and 18K only describe the carbon weave, usually of the outer layer, so don't choose by them.

What the label means

    Try a demo racket before you buy: feel beats any label.

    What is a padel racket made of?

    Every padel racket is built in layers. There are no strings.

    • Core: the thick middle, made of EVA rubber or a softer foam. It decides most of the soft or hard feel.
    • Face skins: thin sheets on both sides, made of fibreglass, carbon or both.
    • Surface: the top finish. Smooth, gritty (sand) or with raised 3D bumps for spin.
    • Frame: a hollow tube around the head and handle, often carbon.
    • Holes: they make the racket lighter and easier to swing.

    Carbon or fibreglass: which padel racket face is better?

    Neither is better for everyone. It depends on your level and your arm.

    What do 3K, 12K and 18K mean on a padel racket?

    The number

    K means thousands of fibres

    Carbon is woven from bundles of very thin fibres. 3K means about 3,000 fibres per bundle, 12K about 12,000 and 18K about 18,000.

    The layer

    Usually only the top sheet

    A racket face has several layers. The K number on the label usually names the outer, visible layer, not the whole face.

    The catch

    Guides don't agree on feel

    Some shops say a higher K feels stiffer. Others say 18K feels softer and springier than 3K. A detailed tech guide says K on its own has little direct effect on play.

    What to do

    Judge the whole racket

    Look at the core, shape, weight and balance first. Two 12K rackets can feel very different. A higher number is not a higher level.

    EVA and foam slowly compress with use, which is one reason an old racket can feel "dead". Test yours with the racket health check.

    Does a rough padel racket give more spin?

    Yes, a little

    Grip on the ball

    A rough face holds the ball a fraction longer, which helps slice on bandejas, víboras and chiquitas. Technique still makes most of the spin.

    Sand

    Gritty finish

    Sand or grit mixed into the top layer. It grips well when new. One racket shop notes the grit can wear smooth over time.

    3D

    Raised bumps

    A pattern moulded into the face. The same shop says it keeps its texture longer than sand. Some rackets use both.

    Smooth

    Clean contact

    Smooth faces favour flat, controlled shots. For a beginner learning to hit cleanly, smooth or a light texture is fine.

    Questions players ask

    Is carbon or fibreglass better for a padel racket?

    Neither is better for everyone. Fibreglass is softer, more forgiving and usually cheaper, which suits beginners and sore arms. Carbon is stiffer and gives more power on fast swings, which suits experienced players.

    What does 3K, 12K or 18K mean on a padel racket?

    It is the number of carbon fibres in each woven bundle: about 3,000, 12,000 or 18,000. It usually describes only the outer layer of the face, so it says little about how the whole racket plays.

    Is 18K carbon better than 12K or 3K?

    Not by itself. Racket guides disagree on whether a higher K feels stiffer or softer, and a detailed tech guide says the K number has little direct effect. The core, shape, weight and balance matter more.

    What is EVA in a padel racket?

    EVA is a rubbery foam used for the core of most padel rackets. Soft EVA feels comfortable and forgiving; hard EVA feels firm and gives more power to fast, clean swings.

    Is a soft or hard padel racket better for beginners?

    Soft. A soft EVA or foam core with a fibreglass face is easier on the arm and forgives mis-hits while you learn.

    Do rough padel rackets give more spin?

    A little. A rough or 3D face grips the ball slightly longer, which helps slice. Technique makes most of the spin. Sandy finishes can wear smoother over time.

    Which padel racket material is best for tennis elbow?

    Racket guides usually suggest a soft core (soft EVA or foam), a fibreglass or soft mixed face, a lighter weight and a low balance. See a doctor or physio if pain lasts.
