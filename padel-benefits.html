Benefits of padel: what it does for your body and mind

What the evidence says about padel for fitness, heart, strength, mood and social life, where the research is still thin, and a quick check of how much your weekly padel counts towards the health guideline.

Quick answer

Padel is a moderate-to-vigorous workout: LTA Padel says most data puts players at about 70 to 85% of their maximum heart rate. Studies link it with better aerobic fitness, sprint speed, grip strength, agility, coordination and mood, and it is easier to start than tennis. Most research is still small and early. Two or three 60 to 90 minute games a week meets the WHO guideline of at least 150 minutes of moderate activity.

Last updated 2026-11-28

Does my padel meet the guideline?

The WHO advises adults to do at least 150 minutes of moderate activity a week (or 75 minutes of vigorous activity), plus muscle-strengthening on 2 days.

Active time is our own estimate: in a social game you rest between points and change ends, so we count 60% of court time for social play, 75% for steady rallies and 85% for competitive play. Competitive play is counted as vigorous (each minute counts double), as the WHO guideline allows. To stay on the safe side we count steady rallies as moderate, although by MET values a normal club match is often vigorous (see the calories page). This is general information, not medical advice.

What are the health benefits of padel?

Benefit 1

Heart and fitness

Players spend much of a game at about 70 to 85% of their maximum heart rate (LTA Padel). That is aerobic training, done in short bursts with rests, which many people find easier to keep up than running.

Benefit 2

Speed, agility and balance

Constant short sprints, stops and turns train agility, coordination and balance. Studies reviewed by National Geographic link padel with gains in sprint speed.

Benefit 3

Strength

Research suggests gains in grip strength and lower-back (lumbar) strength. Padel alone is not full strength training, so add 2 days of exercises: see the padel workout builder.

Benefit 4

Mood and stress

LTA Padel reports reduced stress and better mood from regular play. Early studies also look at brain health markers, but this research is new.

Benefit 5

Thinking and decisions

Fast rallies make you read the ball, the walls and your partner every second. LTA Padel's coach says there is good evidence across racket sports for decision-making benefits.

Benefit 6

Social and easy to start

It is always doubles, the court is small and the serve is underarm, so beginners can rally in their first session. That is why people keep coming back.

Is padel good for older players and people over 50?

Yes, for most people, because you can control the intensity, the court is small and the game is social. LTA Padel notes that about 42% of UK adults over 55 are estimated to be physically inactive, against 29% of all adults, and points to club programmes built for older and returning players. Start with social games, warm up every time and build up slowly. If you have a heart, joint or other health condition, check with your doctor first.

What are the risks, and what does the research not show yet?

Note 1

Injuries happen, but are not common

A systematic review cited by National Geographic found about 3 injuries per 1,000 hours of padel, with elbow problems more common than in many sports. See padel injuries and prevention and the padel elbow check.

Note 2

The research is young

Most padel studies are small and use similar groups of players. Mental health and hormone findings especially are early, so treat big claims with care.

Note 3

It does not replace strength work

Padel trains fitness and agility well, but the guideline still asks for 2 days of muscle-strengthening a week.

Questions players ask

What are the benefits of playing padel?

Better aerobic fitness, agility, coordination, balance and grip strength, plus mood and social benefits. It is also easy to start, so people tend to keep playing.

Is padel a good workout?

Yes. Players spend much of a game at about 70 to 85% of their maximum heart rate, which is moderate to vigorous exercise, done in short bursts.

Is padel better exercise than tennis?

Not better, but easier to start. At beginner and intermediate level it is usually less demanding than tennis, and the intensity is easy to control.

How often should I play padel to stay fit?

Two or three 60 to 90 minute games a week usually meets the WHO guideline of 150 minutes of moderate activity. Add 2 days of strength exercises.

Is padel good for weight loss?

It burns a fair amount of energy and is easy to do often, which helps. Check your number with our padel calories calculator; diet matters too.

Is padel safe for older people?

For most people, yes, because you can control the pace. Warm up, start with social games and check with your doctor if you have a health condition.
