Padel wall rules: was that ball in?

Glass, fence, net, ceiling: tap what your shot touched and see the call, with the ball path drawn on a side view of the court. Then read the wall and fence rules in plain English.

Quick answer

Your shot must bounce on the other team's ground first. After that bounce it may hit their glass or metal fence and stay in play. If it hits their glass or fence before bouncing, you lose the point. On your own side you may play the ball into your own glass, but never into your own metal fence.

What are the wall rules in padel?

The golden rule

Ground first, then walls

Your shot must cross the net and bounce on their ground before it touches anything else on their side. After that bounce, the glass and the fence are both part of the game.

Your side

Your glass is a tool

You can hit the ball into your own glass to send it back over the net. This is a normal shot in padel, often used when the ball gets past you into the corner.

Your side

Your fence is not

Hitting the ball into the metal mesh on your own side loses the point. Only the glass may be used to play the ball back.

Their side

One bounce, then anything

After one bounce on their side, the ball may hit their glass, their fence or both, as many times as it likes. They must hit it before it touches the ground a second time.

Net

Net cord is fine in a rally

In a rally, a ball that clips the net and lands on their side is good. On the serve it is a let. Touching the net or the posts with your body or racket loses the point. More in the net rules checker.

Outside

Out of the court

A ball that bounces on their side and then flies out of the court was a good shot. At clubs that allow it, they may run out and play it back. If not, you win the point.

Can you play the ball outside the court in padel?

What it is

Sometimes a strong smash bounces on your side and flies out over the wall or through the door. At some courts you are allowed to run out of the court and hit the ball back before it bounces a second time. In Spanish this is called a salida de pista.

When it is allowed

Only where the court is built for it: open door gaps and enough clear space outside. If the space isn't there, the club does not allow it, and a ball that bounces and leaves the court simply wins the point for the hitter.

How to play it

Run out through the door, let the ball drop, and hit it back over the wall into the opponents' court. Your feet can be outside the court only when out-of-court play is allowed. Agree on the local rule before you start. Every out-of-court call is on our out-of-court play checker.

Are the wall rules different on the serve?

Glass after the bounce: fine

A serve that bounces in the correct service box and then hits the glass is a good serve, just like in a rally.

Fence after the bounce: fault

A serve that bounces in the box and then hits the metal fence before a second bounce is a fault. In a rally the fence would be fine, so this catches many new players out.

Net, then in: let

A serve that clips the net and lands in the right box is a let and is played again, unless it then hits the fence. All serve calls are on the serve rules checker.

Questions players ask

Can you hit the ball off your own wall in padel?

Yes, off your own glass. You can hit the ball into your glass so it rebounds over the net, as long as it then bounces on the other side before touching their walls. Hitting your own metal fence loses the point.

Is it in if the ball hits the fence in padel?

If the ball bounced on the ground first, yes: it stays in play. If it hit the fence before bouncing on the other side, the player who hit it loses the point.

What happens if the ball goes out of the court in padel?

If it bounced on the opponents' side first, the shot was good. Where out-of-court play is allowed, the opponents may run out and return it before a second bounce. Otherwise the hitter wins the point.

Is a net cord a let in padel?

Only on the serve. During a rally, a ball that touches the net and lands on the other side is in play, and players carry on.

What if the ball gets stuck in the fence in padel?

If it bounced on the opponents' side and then gets stuck in the mesh, goes out through a hole, or stays on top of the wall, the player who hit it wins the point.

Can you touch the net in padel?

No. Touching the net, the posts or the opponents' court with your body, clothes or racket while the ball is in play loses the point.

What if the ball hits the ceiling in padel?

If your shot hits the ceiling or a light before bouncing on the opponents' side, you lose the point.

Can the serve hit the fence in padel?

No. If the serve bounces in the service box and then hits the metal fence before bouncing again, it is a fault. Hitting the glass after the bounce is fine.

Can the ball hit more than one wall in padel?

Yes. After it bounces once on the opponents' side, it may hit the glass, the fence or both, as many times as it likes. They must play it before it touches the ground a second time.
