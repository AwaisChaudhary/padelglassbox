Padel injuries: where it hurts and how to prevent it

Tap the part of the body that worries you to see how often it shows up in padel, which moves load it and the habits that help. Then run the two-minute prevention check.

Quick answer

The elbow is the most often reported padel injury, followed by the knee, shoulder and lower back, and most padel injuries are tendon or muscle problems, not broken bones. The habits that help most: a 10 to 15 minute warm-up, building up playing time slowly, court shoes, a relaxed grip, coaching on technique and stopping when something hurts. This page is general information, not medical advice.

How common are padel injuries?

About 3 per 1,000 hours

A 2023 review of 8 studies with 2,022 padel players (BMJ Open Sport & Exercise Medicine) found about 3 injuries per 1,000 hours of training and about 8 per 1,000 matches.

Most players get one

Between 40% and 95% of the players in those studies said they had been injured at some point. Most of the studies asked players to remember, so treat these as rough numbers.

Tendons and muscles

Most reported injuries were to tendons (like tennis elbow) or muscles (like calf strains), not bones. These are often overuse injuries that build up over time.

What is padel elbow, and what can you do about it?

What it feels like

Pain on the outside of the elbow. It can be mild or constant, and it gets worse when you grip, lift or turn your wrist. The NHS lists tenderness and trouble fully straightening the arm as other signs.

Why it happens

The NHS describes tennis elbow as a problem with the tendons of the forearm after lots of gripping and twisting. In padel you grip and use your wrist on almost every shot.

Looking after it at home

NHS advice: rest from what makes it worse, use painkillers such as paracetamol or an anti-inflammatory gel, and use a hot or cold pack wrapped in a towel for up to 20 minutes every 2 to 3 hours. A forearm strap or brace from a pharmacy may help.

When to see a GP

If the pain has not got better after 2 weeks of rest and self-care. A GP may suggest physiotherapy if home treatment has not helped after about 6 weeks.

How long it lasts

It often gets better within a few weeks of rest, but the NHS says it can last more than a year. Coming back too fast is a common reason it returns.

Does the racket matter?

Many coaches and shops suggest a lighter, less head-heavy racket and a thicker, fresh grip for sore elbows. This makes sense but has not been proven in studies. Compare specs with the weight and balance decoder.

How do you prevent padel injuries?

1. Warm up every time

A short jog, moving stretches, leg exercises and easy rallies. Not warming up properly is one of the common causes of sports injuries listed by NI Direct. Use the warm-up timer.

2. Build up slowly

Don't go from one game a week to five. Pushing your body past your current fitness is another common cause of sports injuries.

3. The right shoes

Padel or court shoes with a sole that suits your court. Running shoes are made for straight lines, not side steps. See the padel shoes guide.

4. Learn the technique

Coaching on the bandeja, volleys and glass shots takes load off your elbow, shoulder and back. Plan practice with the drills planner.

5. Fresh balls and grip

Flat balls need more effort to hit and a slippery grip makes you squeeze. Check with the dead ball checker.

6. Stop when it hurts

Playing on with pain can cause more damage and slow your recovery. Rest, and get advice if it doesn't settle.

Questions players ask

What is the most common injury in padel?

The elbow. In a 2023 review of padel studies, the elbow was the most often reported injury area, mostly tennis elbow (pain on the outer elbow), followed by the knee, shoulder and lower back.

What is padel elbow?

Padel elbow is the name players use for tennis elbow (lateral epicondylitis): pain on the outside of the elbow from the forearm tendons, made worse by gripping and turning the wrist.

Can I keep playing padel with tennis elbow?

It is better to rest from what makes it worse. The NHS advises rest, painkillers or gel and a hot or cold pack, and seeing a GP if it hasn't improved after 2 weeks. Playing through pain can slow recovery.

How long does padel elbow take to heal?

It often improves within a few weeks of rest, but the NHS says tennis elbow can last more than a year. Build back up slowly when it feels better.

Is padel bad for your knees?

Not by itself, but quick stops and turns load the knee, and knee injuries are the second most reported in padel studies. Court shoes, a proper warm-up and good footwork help.

How can I prevent padel injuries?

Warm up for 10 to 15 minutes, build up playing time slowly, wear court shoes, keep a fresh grip and relaxed hold, get coaching on technique, take rest days and stop when you feel pain.

Is padel more dangerous than tennis?

There is not enough research to say. The 2023 review found about 3 injuries per 1,000 hours of padel training, but its authors say comparisons with other racket sports should be made carefully.
