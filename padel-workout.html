Padel workout builder: strength for your legs, core and arm.

Choose home or gym, what you want to work on and how long you have. You get a circuit of padel-focused exercises with a work and rest timer that beeps at every change. No account, no app, just press start.

Quick answer

The best gym exercises for padel train the legs (squats, lunges, side lunges, calf raises), the core and rotation (planks, side planks, woodchops, Pallof press) and the shoulder and forearm (rows, band external rotation, wrist curls). Health guidelines from the WHO and the NHS say adults should do muscle-strengthening work on at least 2 days a week; two 20 to 30 minute sessions on days you don't play is a simple way to do it.

    How often should padel players do strength training?

    The WHO's physical activity guidelines and the NHS both say adults should do muscle-strengthening activities that work all the major muscle groups on at least 2 days a week, on top of their aerobic activity (and padel counts as aerobic activity). For most club players, a simple week looks like this:

    DayPlan (example)
    MondayStrength 25 min (full body)
    TuesdayPadel match
    WednesdayRest or easy walk
    ThursdayStrength 25 min (legs or core focus) or a solo practice session
    SaturdayPadel match or lesson

    Our own suggestion: keep hard leg sessions at least a day away from a match, so your legs are fresh on court. Start with body weight, and add load only when 12 clean reps feel easy.

    How many sets and reps?

    Level 1

    New to strength work

    40 seconds work, 20 seconds rest, 2 or 3 rounds. Body weight only. Move slowly and stop 2 or 3 reps before form breaks.

    Level 2

    You train often

    45 seconds work, 15 seconds rest, 3 or 4 rounds, or 3 sets of 8 to 12 reps with a weight that makes the last 2 reps hard (NHS guidance: repeat until it is hard to do one more).

    Level 3

    Forearm and shoulder care

    Light weights, slow lowering over 3 seconds. These are about tissue health, not lifting heavy. Daily is fine if they feel good.

    Can strength training help prevent padel injuries?

    Stronger muscles and tendons handle load better, and sports bodies and physios recommend strength work as part of injury prevention, together with a proper warm-up and building up your playing time gradually. It is not a guarantee. For the common padel problems (elbow, calf, knee, lower back and shoulder) and when to see a professional, read padel injuries and prevention. Forearm pain on the outside of the elbow has its own page: padel elbow and the arm-load check.

    Questions players ask

    What are the best gym exercises for padel?

    Squats, lunges and side lunges for the legs, calf raises, planks, side planks and woodchops for the core and rotation, and rows, band external rotation and wrist curls for the shoulder and forearm.

    How often should I strength train for padel?

    At least 2 days a week, which is what the WHO and NHS recommend for all adults. Short 20 to 30 minute sessions on days you don't play work well.

    Can I do a padel workout at home with no equipment?

    Yes. Squats, lunges, side lunges, calf raises, glute bridges, planks, side planks, push-ups, Y-T raises and wrist curls with a water bottle need no gym.

    Should I lift weights before or after playing padel?

    Many players keep hard leg sessions on a different day from matches so their legs are fresh. If you must do both on one day, play first and keep the strength work short.

    Which exercises help with padel elbow?

    Slow wrist curls and reverse wrist curls with a light weight, plus grip and shoulder work, are commonly used. See a physio if pain lasts.

    How long should a padel workout be?

    Fifteen to thirty-five minutes is enough for most club players: a short warm-up, a circuit of 6 to 10 exercises for 2 to 4 rounds, then a stretch.
