The chiquita in padel: what it is, how to hit it, and when to play it.

The chiquita is a soft, low ball from the back that drops at the net players' feet, so your pair can take the net. Use the picker to see if this ball is a chiquita or a lob, learn the five steps, and see what to do when someone plays one at you.

Quick answer

A chiquita (say chee-KEE-ta, Spanish for 'small one') is a slow, low shot played from the middle or back of the court so it lands at the feet of the opponents at the net. It makes them volley up, so you and your partner can move forward and attack the next ball. Play it soft, from a ball you can reach comfortably, when the other pair expects a lob; never from right against the back glass.

How do you hit a chiquita?

Five steps. Forehand or backhand; many players find the backhand easier to slice.

Step 1

Pick the right ball

A ball you can reach comfortably, usually one that bounces in the middle of your half. Not one that pins you against the back glass.

Step 2

Prepare like a lob

Get under the ball with small side steps, knees bent, the racket face a little open. The other pair should think a lob is coming.

Step 3

Play it soft and slow

Use a gentle, controlled swing. Placement, not power. A fast chiquita gives them a ball to volley back hard.

Step 4

Aim at the feet

The target is the net player's feet, or the gap between the two of them, so the ball dips below the net on their side.

Step 5

Move in at once

Follow the ball forward straight away with your partner. Do not stop to watch: the chiquita's whole point is to win back the net.

When should you play a chiquita?

When they expect a lob

If the net pair starts to step back for your lobs, a chiquita at their feet catches them out.

When they volley well from mid-court

A pair that is comfortable volleying high balls has to bend and lift a low ball. That slows them down.

When you want the net

The chiquita is not a winner. It is a way to swap places: they hit up, you come forward.

Down the middle or cross-court

Through the middle is the safest. Cross-court at a player's body works too, but only from a good position, or a fast reply at your body can win them the point.

Common chiquita mistakes

Too fast

A hard ball at net players is a gift. Slow it down so it drops at their feet.

Too high

If it floats above the net they can attack it. Keep it low; aim to clear the net by a little.

From too deep

Played with your back at the glass, you cannot get forward in time. Lob from there instead.

Standing to watch

If you wait, a good volley leaves you stuck in the middle. Go forward as you hit.

Only chiquitas

Use it with the lob. Once they step in to cover the chiquita, the lob goes over them.

How do you deal with a chiquita?

When you are at the net and a low ball comes at your feet.

Bend, don't attack

You are below the net, so you cannot hit down. Bend your knees, keep the racket head up and play a soft, low volley back.

Play it deep or soft

Either a slow, deep volley down the middle to keep them back, or a soft drop at the feet of the player coming in. More in the volley guide (try the low volley in the picker).

Don't stand too close

About two to three metres from the net gives you time to bend for the chiquita and still turn for a lob.

Questions players ask

What is a chiquita in padel?

A slow, low ball played from the back half of the court that drops at the feet of the players at the net. It makes them hit up, so your pair can move forward.

How do you pronounce chiquita?

Chee-KEE-ta. It is Spanish and means 'small one'.

When should you play a chiquita in padel?

From a ball you can reach comfortably in the middle of your half, when the net pair expects a lob or is standing back a little. Not when you are pinned against the back glass.

Where should a chiquita land?

At the net player's feet or in the gap between the two net players, low over the net so they have to volley up.

Chiquita or lob: which is better?

Use both. The lob sends them back; the chiquita brings them in or makes them bend. Mixing them is what makes each one work.

How do you return a chiquita?

Bend your knees, keep the racket head up and play a soft, low volley, deep down the middle or at the feet of the player coming in. Do not try to hit down on a ball below the net.
