How to hit a lob in padel, and what to do when you face one.

The lob is the shot that wins the net back. Here is how to hit it, where to aim, a lob picker that tells you which lob to play in your situation, and a second picker for the shot to hit when the lob comes at you.

Quick answer

To hit a padel lob, get low, open the racket face and swing from low to high with your legs, finishing with the racket above your head. Aim for the ball to bounce past the service line but before the back glass, and play it cross-court for more margin. A good lob pushes the net pair back and gives your team time to take the net.

Where should a padel lob land?

The target zone, as LTA Padel coaches it.

Past the service line

The ball should bounce between the service line and the back glass. That pushes the net pair all the way back and makes a strong smash hard.

Before the back glass

If it hits the back glass without bouncing, it is out. Land it short of the glass and let it come off the wall slowly.

High enough

A high lob clears the players' reach and loses speed off the glass, so it stays close to the wall and is hard to play.

Cross-court first

The diagonal is the longest line on the court, so a cross-court lob has more room to land in. See the court sizes in court dimensions.

How do you hit a lob in padel?

Four steps. Use a continental grip, as in our grip guide.

Step 1

Get under the ball

Prepare like any groundstroke, but bend your knees and get low so you meet the ball below its middle.

Step 2

Open the racket face

Tilt the face up a little more than for a drive. The open face gives height, not a big swing.

Step 3

Swing low to high

Move the racket up through the ball and push up with your legs. Do not jump or lift your whole body.

Step 4

Finish high

End with the racket above your head, pointing where you want the ball to go. A high finish gives a deep lob.

Practise it: balance the ball on your racket, drop it and tap it up, then tap it up in rallies with a partner before you try full lobs. Then play "two up, two back": one pair at the net, one at the back, first to 10 points, swap every two points. More ideas in the drills planner.

Common lob mistakes

Lobbing off balance

A lob hit while stretched or running usually lands short. When you are in trouble, a higher, safer lob is better than a fast one.

Lifting the whole body

Jumping up for height makes the ball fly long. Bend the knees, stay down and let the legs push up.

Too short

A lob that lands before the service line is an easy smash. Aim past the service line.

Too flat

A low lob is easy to intercept and bounces hard off the back glass, giving your opponents an easy ball.

Always down the line

Cross-court is longer, so you have more room for error. Use down the line as a surprise.

When should you lob in padel?

When both opponents are at the net

The lob is the main way to move them back so your pair can take the net. Learn the rest of the plan in padel tactics.

When you need time

A high defensive lob buys time to get back into position after a hard ball. See where to stand.

Against tired or slow players

Lobs make the net pair turn and run back again and again. Over a match, that tires them out.

Not when they are both back

If both opponents are already at the back, a lob just gives them the net. Play a low ball or a chiquita at their feet instead. See padel shots.

Questions players ask

How do you hit a good lob in padel?

Get low, open the racket face and swing from low to high, pushing up with your legs and finishing with the racket above your head. Aim past the service line and before the back glass.

Where should a padel lob land?

Between the service line and the back glass on your opponents' side. Cross-court gives you the most room.

Is the lob an attacking or defensive shot?

Both. A high, safe lob defends and buys time. A lower, faster lob over a net player who is too close can win the point or the net.

Should I lob cross-court or down the line?

Mostly cross-court, because the diagonal is longer and gives more margin. Down the line works as a surprise.

What do you do against a lob in padel?

Turn sideways early and move back. Short lobs can be smashed or hit with a víbora; middle lobs suit a bandeja or víbora; deep lobs are often best let past onto the back glass and played from there.

Why do my lobs go out?

Usually you lift your whole body, swing too hard, or hit while off balance. Stay low, use your legs, slow the swing and aim higher rather than longer.
