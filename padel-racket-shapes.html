Padel racket shapes: round, teardrop or diamond?

The shape of a padel racket decides where the sweet spot sits and where the weight is, and that changes control, power and how kind it is to your arm. See the three shapes to scale, compare them side by side, and answer five questions to find the shape that suits you.

Quick answer

Round padel rackets have a large sweet spot in the middle and a low balance, so they give the most control and suit beginners and defensive players. Diamond rackets have a small sweet spot near the top and a high balance, so they give the most power and suit experienced attacking players. Teardrop rackets sit in between and suit most improving and all-round players. If your elbow or shoulder gets sore, stay with round or a low-balance teardrop.

Last updated 2026-11-28

Round

Big sweet spot in the middle, low balance. The most control and the most forgiving.

Teardrop

Medium sweet spot a little higher, medium balance. A mix of control and power.

Diamond

Small sweet spot near the top, high balance. The most power, the least forgiving.

Balance is measured from the bottom of the handle. Ranges are from the Tennis Warehouse racket guide; brands measure slightly differently, so treat them as a guide. Check any racket's numbers with the balance decoder.

Does shape matter more than weight?

Shape, balance and weight work together. Most rackets weigh about 350 to 380 g; HEAD says beginners and intermediates usually prefer about 340 to 360 g and tournament players about 360 to 375 g. A light diamond can feel easier than a heavy round racket, and a teardrop with a low balance plays closer to a round one. Pick the shape for your game, then the weight for your body: the racket weight calculator gives a number in grams.

When should you change racket shape?

Change 1

Round to teardrop

When you hit cleanly in the middle of the face most of the time, play a few times a week and want more depth on overheads.

Change 2

Teardrop to diamond

When you finish most points at the net with smashes and bandejas, hit the sweet spot reliably and have no arm pain.

Change 3

Back to round

If your elbow or shoulder starts to hurt, or you miss more than you win with a head-heavy racket, go back to a lower balance.

Change 4

Try before you buy

Many clubs rent or demo rackets. Play a full session with a new shape before you spend; the first few games always feel strange.

Questions players ask

Which padel racket shape is best for beginners?

Round. It has the biggest sweet spot in the middle of the face and a low balance, so mis-hits still go over and it is easy on the arm.

What is the difference between round, teardrop and diamond padel rackets?

Round gives the most control with a big central sweet spot. Diamond gives the most power with a small sweet spot near the top. Teardrop is in between.

Is a teardrop padel racket good for intermediate players?

Yes, it is the usual next step for improving and all-round players who want a little more power without losing much control.

Is a diamond padel racket bad for tennis elbow?

It carries more risk. Diamond rackets are usually head-heavy, and racket guides warn that a high balance can strain the elbow in untrained arms. Stay round or low-balance teardrop if you have pain, and see a doctor or physio if it lasts.

What balance does a round padel racket have?

Usually low, about 250 to 260 mm from the bottom of the handle. Teardrops are about 265 to 270 mm and diamonds about 270 to 280 mm or more.

Do professionals use diamond rackets?

Many attacking pros do, but plenty use teardrop or even round rackets. Shape is a personal choice, not a sign of level.
