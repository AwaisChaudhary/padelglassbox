Padel etiquette: the unwritten rules of the court.

What to do (and not do) in the warm-up, during points, between points and after the match. Test yourself with 10 real situations.

Quick answer

The main padel etiquette rules: arrive a few minutes early and leave the court on time; feed easy balls in the warm-up and don't smash; check the receiver is ready before you serve; call your own double bounces and touches, and give the point away if in doubt; roll or toss spare balls gently to the server; stop for a let if a ball rolls on from another court; change ends quickly after odd games; don't celebrate the other pair's mistakes; and tap rackets with all three players at the end.

Padel etiquette checklist

The same rules, by moment of the game.

Before you play

  • Arrive about 5 minutes early so you start on time.
  • Wear padel or clay/omni shoes, not running shoes. See padel shoes.
  • Take off watches and jewellery that could hurt someone.
  • Take turns bringing a new tube of balls.

In the warm-up

  • Hit easy balls to where the other player can reach them.
  • No smashes or winners.
  • Share the time: swap net and back after a few minutes.

During points

  • Check the receiver is ready before you serve.
  • Call your own double bounces, touches and net touches.
  • If in doubt, the point goes to the other pair.
  • Call "mine" or "yours" early with your partner.

Between points

  • Roll or gently toss balls to the server.
  • Don't rush or slow the game down.
  • Change ends quickly after odd games.
  • Say the score before you serve.

After the match

  • Tap rackets or shake hands with all three players.
  • Collect your balls, bottles and rubbish.
  • Leave on time for the next booking.
  • Thank the organiser.

Safety first

  • Stop for a let if a ball from another court comes onto yours.
  • Never hit the ball hard at a player who is not ready.
  • Keep the court door closed during play.

Questions players ask

What are the unwritten rules of padel?

Arrive early and leave on time, play easy balls in the warm-up, check the receiver is ready, call your own double bounces and touches, return balls gently, change ends quickly, don't celebrate opponents' errors, and thank all three players at the end.

Can you smash in the padel warm-up?

It is poor etiquette. The warm-up is for both pairs to find rhythm, so hit easy balls the other player can return.

Who calls double bounces in padel?

In social games with no umpire, the player who hit the ball calls it. If nobody is sure, the custom is to give the point to the other pair.

What happens if a ball from another court comes onto yours?

Stop and play a let (replay the point). It is a safety risk to keep playing. Send the ball back when the other court's point is over.

Do you shake hands after padel?

Yes. Tap rackets or shake hands at the net with your partner and both opponents, and thank the organiser.

What shoes should I wear for padel?

Padel shoes or clay/omni-court tennis shoes with a herringbone sole. Running shoes slip on the sand-filled turf and can damage it.
