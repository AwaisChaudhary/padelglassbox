Padel racket for kids: which size and weight?

Junior padel rackets differ mainly in weight, not length. Answer five quick questions about the child to see the weight band to look for, the right shape and core, and whether they are ready for an adult racket.

Quick answer

Choose by weight. As a rough guide: about 210 to 260 g for ages 3 to 6, 260 to 300 g for 7 to 9, 300 to 330 g for 10 to 12, and 330 to 355 g for 13 to 14. Pick a round shape with a soft core and a fibreglass face, use the wrist cord, and move to a light adult racket (about 350 to 360 g) at around 12 to 14, when the child is strong enough.

For comparison, most adult rackets weigh about 355 to 375 g. Check the weight printed on the racket or the shop page: some rackets sold as "junior" still weigh 330 to 350 g, which is heavy for a young child.

Are junior padel rackets shorter?

A little, but weight matters more

Unlike tennis, padel rackets don't come in many lengths. Older junior rackets are often only about a finger shorter than an adult racket. The big difference is weight, and a thinner handle for small hands.

The adult limit

The rules allow a racket up to 45.5 cm long and 38 mm thick. Junior rackets are smaller or thinner than that. See all the sizes on our racket weight and balance page.

Don't cut down an adult racket

A shortened adult racket is still heavy and stiff, so it tires a young arm. A racket made for juniors is lighter all over.

How do I know if a padel racket is too heavy for my child?

SignWhat to do
Their arm, elbow or shoulder is sore after playingThe most common sign the racket is too heavy or too stiff. Go lighter and softer, and rest. If pain lasts, see a doctor or physio. More in padel injuries.
The racket head drops after a few shotsTheir arm is tired. A lighter racket lets them keep good form for longer.
They are always late on the ballA heavy racket is slow to swing and hard to get ready at the net.
They hold it with two hands for most shotsOften a sign it is too heavy. Try one band lighter.
It feels too light and they say it "flaps"Too light is less of a problem than too heavy. Move up one band when they grow or get stronger.

Which shape, core and face suit a child?

Shape

Round

A round racket has its sweet spot low and in the middle, so off-centre hits still go over. Teardrop and diamond shapes move the sweet spot higher and are harder to control. Keep those for strong teenagers.

Core

Soft foam

A soft EVA or foam core gives an easy, comfortable feel and helps a light swing send the ball deep. Hard cores are for strong adult players.

Face

Fibreglass

Fibreglass faces are more flexible and cheaper. Stiff carbon faces are for older, stronger players. Learn more in racket specs explained.

Grip

Thin handle

Junior rackets have thinner handles. An overgrip can make a handle thicker as their hand grows, but nothing makes a thick handle thinner. See how to hold a padel racket.

At what age can kids start playing padel?

From about 3 years old

HEAD says children can start hitting first shots from about age three, once they are strong enough to hold the racket. LTA Padel says padel lets children of all ages and abilities join in.

Use the wrist cord

The cord on the handle should go round the wrist every time. It stops the racket flying out of a small hand and hitting someone. The FIP rules say every player must wear it.

Coaching and junior events

In Great Britain, LTA Padel runs youth competitions for all ages and levels. They use a simple, fast format, usually last about half a day, and earn LTA ranking points. Ask your club about junior sessions with an accredited coach.

When should a child move to an adult padel racket?

There is no fixed age. HEAD suggests around 12 or 13; Padel Browser says usually 12 to 14. It depends on strength and technique more than birthdays. Signs they are ready:

  • They are close to adult height and can swing a 330 to 355 g racket without tiring.
  • They play every week and their arm never hurts afterwards.
  • They hit with good form at the end of a session, not only at the start.

Start with a light, round adult racket of about 350 to 360 g. Then use the racket finder to choose the next one.

Questions players ask

What size padel racket should a child use?

Choose by weight more than length: about 210 to 260 g for ages 3 to 6, 260 to 300 g for 7 to 9, 300 to 330 g for 10 to 12 and 330 to 355 g for 13 to 14. Adjust for the child's height and strength.

How heavy should a junior padel racket be?

Most junior rackets weigh between about 260 and 330 g. Younger children need the lighter end. Adult rackets usually weigh about 355 to 375 g.

At what age can a child use an adult padel racket?

Usually around 12 to 14, when they are strong enough. Start with a light, round adult racket of about 350 to 360 g.

What shape padel racket is best for kids?

Round. It has a low, central sweet spot that forgives off-centre hits. Teardrop and diamond rackets are harder to control.

At what age can kids start padel?

Children can start hitting first shots from about age three, once they can hold a light racket. Coaching sessions for juniors are common at clubs.

Can a child use a cut-down adult padel racket?

It is better not to. A shortened adult racket is still heavy and stiff and tires a young arm. Junior rackets are lighter and softer all over.

Are junior padel rackets shorter than adult rackets?

Only a little. Older junior rackets are often about a finger shorter than an adult racket. The main difference is a lighter weight and a thinner handle.
